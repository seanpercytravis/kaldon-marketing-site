## When agencies win

### Cross-channel strategy and positioning

AI platforms are weak at holistic growth strategy. They execute well-defined tasks. They do not tell you what to prioritize or how to position against competitors.

If you are launching into a crowded category and need differentiation strategy, hire an agency. If you are expanding from Amazon to Walmart and DTC simultaneously and need a phased rollout plan, hire an agency. If you are repositioning an existing brand and need customer research and messaging architecture, hire an agency.

Cost: $5,000 to $25,000 for a strategic engagement. $10,000 to $50,000 per month for ongoing advisory retainers.

ROI: hard to measure directly, but the difference between a differentiated brand and a me-too product is often 2x to 5x margin.

### High-risk, high-budget launches

If you are launching a product with $50,000+ in inventory, $20,000+ in paid media budget, and regulatory or compliance risk (supplements, beauty, kids products), do not DIY it with AI.

Agencies bring:

- Risk mitigation (claims review, FDA/FTC compliance, platform policy vetting)
- Media buying expertise (incrementality testing, attribution modeling, budget allocation)
- Crisis management (negative reviews, account suspensions, supply chain disruptions)

Cost: $15,000 to $100,000+ for a full-service launch.

When to pay it: when the downside of failure is greater than the cost of the agency.

## The accountability question

Who is on the hook when AI goes wrong?

If an AI platform generates a product claim that violates FDA rules, is that the platform's fault or yours? If an AI agent mis-prices your inventory, who pays for the loss?

This is the newest concern in 2026. Buyers are asking agencies and platforms to define risk and liability clearly.

Agencies typically include:

- Claims review and compliance vetting
- Account health monitoring
- Crisis response (negative reviews, suspensions)

AI platforms typically include:

- Guardrails and filters (e.g., Kaldon flags high-risk claims and requires manual review)
- Simulation and testing (e.g., Azoma's digital twin simulator for retail AI)
- Kill switches and escalation thresholds

But ultimate accountability still sits with the brand. If you are in a regulated category (supplements, beauty, kids, CBD), you need either a compliance-focused agency or a platform with strong guardrails plus legal review.

Kaldon flags high-risk language in the Create phase and requires manual approval before export. But we are not a law firm. If you are launching a supplement, hire a regulatory consultant or agency to review output.
