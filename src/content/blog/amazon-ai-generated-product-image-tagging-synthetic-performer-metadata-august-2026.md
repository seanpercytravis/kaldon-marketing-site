## Amazon's New AI-Generated People Tagging Rule: What It Is and Why It Exists

On July 23, 2026, Amazon announced mandatory metadata tagging for photorealistic AI-generated people in all seller-uploaded images and videos. The requirement took effect August 15, 2026, and applies retroactively to existing content. Sellers must embed the exact keyword `contains-synthetic-performer` in the XMP `dc:subject` metadata field of any image or video file containing a realistic AI-generated human before uploading. Amazon then surfaces a disclosure to shoppers indicating the presence of AI-generated people.

The rule is a direct response to New York's Synthetic Performer Disclosure Law (effective June 9, 2026) and California's August 2, 2026 AI transparency deadline. These state laws require clear disclosure when AI-generated humans replace real actors in commercial content. Amazon's metadata approach operationalizes compliance for the 2.3 million third-party sellers on the platform, pushing the tagging burden upstream into asset production workflows.

### What Must Be Tagged

The requirement is narrow. Only three conditions trigger mandatory tagging:

1. The image or video contains a **person**.
2. The person is **photorealistic** (reads as a real human, not a cartoon or illustration).
3. The person is **generated by AI** (fully synthetic, not a real human who was photographed and then edited).

If all three are true, the file must carry `contains-synthetic-performer` in its metadata before upload.

### What Is Exempt

- **Real people**, even if the image was AI-edited (background removal, lighting adjustments, upscaling, object removal).
- **Product-only images** with no people.
- **Non-photorealistic AI figures**: cartoons, illustrations, stylized or abstract humanoid forms.
- **Fictional characters** from films, TV shows, or video games.
- **AI-generated animals, plants, objects, or scenery** with no human figures.

A real model photographed in a studio and composited into an AI-generated room does not require the tag. An AI-generated photorealistic model standing in a real room does.

### Where the Requirement Applies

The rule covers all buyer-facing media uploaded to Amazon:

- **Product listing images** (main image and secondary images).
- **A+ Content** images and videos.
- **Brand Story** and **Amazon Stores** images and videos.
- **Advertising creatives** (Sponsored Brands video, display ads, DSP assets).

It applies globally across all Amazon marketplaces, not just the United States.

## Step-by-Step Tagging Workflow

Amazon requires the keyword `contains-synthetic-performer` to be written into the **XMP `dc:subject` field** of the image or video file. This is an IPTC-standard metadata location that Amazon's ingestion system reads during upload. The tag must be present in the file itself, not added via Seller Central UI (with one exception for A+ Content, covered below).

### Windows (10 and 11)

1. Right-click the image file.
2. Select **Properties**.
3. Go to the **Details** tab.
4. Scroll to the **Tags** field (not Subject; Amazon reads from the Tags field on Windows, which maps to `dc:subject`).
5. Type `contains-synthetic-performer` (exact spelling, lowercase, hyphenated).
6. Click **OK**.

### macOS

1. Open the image in **Preview**.
2. Go to **Tools** → **Show Inspector**.
3. Click the **Keywords** tab (the "i" icon).
4. In the **Keywords** field, add `contains-synthetic-performer`.
5. Close the Inspector. The change saves automatically.

### Bulk Tagging with ExifTool (Windows, macOS, Linux)

For agencies, brands with large catalogs, or automated pipelines:

1. Install [ExifTool](https://exiftool.org/).
2. Navigate to the directory containing images.
3. Run:

bash
exiftool -overwrite_original -ext jpg -ext jpeg -ext png -XMP-dc:Subject+="contains-synthetic-performer" .


This command adds the keyword to all JPEG and PNG files in the current directory without creating backup copies. For video files, use `-ext mp4 -ext mov`.

### Adobe Tools (Photoshop, Lightroom, Bridge)

1. Open the file in Photoshop or Bridge.
2. Go to **File** → **File Info** (Photoshop) or **Metadata** panel (Bridge).
3. Under **IPTC Extension** or **Keywords**, add `contains-synthetic-performer`.
4. Save.

Adobe tools write to the XMP `dc:subject` field by default when keywords are added.

### A+ Content Exception: UI-Level Tagging

For A+ Content only, Amazon offers an alternative. When uploading images or videos in **A+ Content Manager** → **Creative Assets**:

1. Upload the file.
2. Check the box labeled **"This image contains AI-generated people"**.
3. Amazon applies the `contains-synthetic-performer` metadata server-side.

This option does not exist for listing images, Brand Story, Stores, or ads. Those require file-level tagging before upload.

## Enforcement Timeline and Penalties

Amazon's policy became mandatory on **August 15, 2026**. The company requires retroactive tagging of all existing content. Sellers with AI-generated people in live listings, A+ modules, or ads uploaded before August 15 must add the metadata or replace the assets.

Amazon has not published a specific grace period, but Seller Forums discussions from late July and early August 2026 report that policy violation warnings began appearing in Seller Central accounts starting August 7. Non-compliant listings risk:

- **Policy violation strikes**.
- **ASIN suppression** (listing removed from search until corrected).
- **Account suspension** for repeated violations.

Separately, sellers operating in New York or California face legal exposure under state synthetic-performer laws. New York's law allows fines up to **$5,000 per day per violation** for covered entities that fail to disclose AI-generated people in ads. While Amazon is the primary regulated party in most cases, sellers using AI-generated models in external advertising (social ads, influencer partnerships, email) that link to Amazon listings should ensure those assets also carry appropriate disclosures.

## Common Questions and Troubleshooting

### Do I need to tag every AI-generated image?

No. Only images containing **photorealistic AI-generated people**. AI-generated product shots, backgrounds, or non-human elements do not require the tag.

### What if my model is real but the background is AI-generated?

No tag required. The rule applies only when the person is synthetic.

### What if the AI-generated person is small or in the background?

Amazon's policy does not specify a size or prominence threshold. If a photorealistic AI-generated human is visible anywhere in the frame (main subject, background pedestrian, distant figure), the conservative interpretation is to tag.

### Does the tag apply to heavily AI-edited real people?

No. If the person started as a real photograph (even if you used AI to change lighting, remove objects, swap backgrounds, or upscale), the tag is not required. The rule applies only to fully synthetic humans.

### Can I use a different keyword or additional keywords?

Amazon specifically requires `contains-synthetic-performer` (exact spelling, lowercase, hyphenated). You can add other keywords to the same metadata field, but the Amazon-required keyword must be present.

### What if I already uploaded images without the tag?

You must either:

1. Download the image, add the metadata, and re-upload (replacing the existing image), or
2. Replace the image with a version that does not contain AI-generated people.

Amazon does not currently offer an in-platform metadata editor for listing images.

### Does this apply to videos?

Yes. Product videos, A+ Content videos, and ad videos containing photorealistic AI-generated people must carry the `contains-synthetic-performer` tag in their XMP metadata.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where exactly do I add the contains-synthetic-performer keyword?

On Windows, right-click the image → Properties → Details → Tags field. On macOS, open in Preview → Tools → Show Inspector → Keywords. For bulk tagging, use ExifTool with the command `exiftool -XMP-dc:Subject+="contains-synthetic-performer" image.jpg`. The keyword must be in the XMP dc:subject metadata field.

### Do I need to tag AI-edited images of real people?

No. The tag is required only for photorealistic AI-generated people (fully synthetic humans). If you photographed a real person and used AI to edit the background, lighting, or other elements, no tag is required.

### What happens if I don't tag an image with AI-generated people?

Amazon may suppress the listing (remove it from search), issue a policy violation, or suspend your account for repeated violations. Sellers in New York and California also face potential state-law fines up to $5,000 per day for non-compliant AI-generated people in ads.

### Does the tagging requirement apply to A+ Content and ads?

Yes. All buyer-facing media on Amazon (listing images, A+ Content, Brand Story, Stores, and advertising creatives) must carry the tag if they contain photorealistic AI-generated people. For A+ Content, you can tag at upload via the "AI-generated people" checkbox instead of editing file metadata.
