## Why Your Amazon Listing Is Not Selling: The Four Failure Modes

Your listing went live. You set up PPC. You followed the optimization checklist. And still, zero sales. Or sales that suddenly dropped to near zero after a title edit, a pricing change, or no change at all.

In the last 30 days, Amazon seller forums and Reddit threads have been filling up with variations of the same question: "My listing is live but I have had no orders for 15 days," "Sales stopped all of a sudden," and "Listing not showing up in search." The most common response is a generic optimization checklist: fix your keywords, adjust your price, run more ads. But optimization tactics only work if you have the right product, the right traffic, and the right platform status. If you have a demand problem, no amount of bullet-point rewriting will save you. If you have a suppression problem, PPC spend is wasted.

Most content on "Amazon listing not selling" assumes the product has demand and the listing just needs better copy or images. That assumption costs sellers thousands of dollars in wasted ad spend, freelance fees, and months of iteration on a product the market does not want. This diagnostic framework separates the four failure modes so you know which lever to pull.

**The four failure modes are:**

1. **Demand problem** (the market does not want this product at this price point)
2. **Traffic problem** (the product has demand, but buyers cannot find the listing)
3. **Conversion problem** (traffic is arriving, but the listing does not convert)
4. **Platform suppression** (Amazon is actively hiding, throttling, or de-prioritizing the listing)

You diagnose in that order. If you have no demand, traffic fixes will not help. If you have demand but no traffic, conversion optimization is premature. If you have traffic and demand but the listing still does not convert, then you optimize. If none of those three explain the failure, you check for suppression.

## Step 1: Run the Demand Test First

Before you spend another dollar on PPC or hire a listing copywriter, confirm that buyers are actually searching for and purchasing products like yours. The demand test answers one question: is there proven buyer intent for this product at this price, or are you trying to create demand from scratch?

**How to run the demand test:**

- **Check category Best Seller Rank movement.** If the top 20 products in your subcategory are moving 50+ units per day and your listing is moving zero, you likely have a traffic or conversion problem, not a demand problem. If the top 20 are moving 2 units per day, you have a demand problem.
- **Run a reverse ASIN lookup on your top 3 competitors.** Tools like Helium 10, Jungle Scout, or [Kaldon's Discover phase](https://kaldon.io/features) will show you which keywords are driving traffic to those listings. If those keywords have search volume above 1,000 per month and the competitor listings are converting, demand exists. If search volume is under 500 and competitors are not converting either, demand is soft.
- **Check Sponsored Product benchmark CTR and CVR.** Amazon's advertising console shows category-level CTR and CVR benchmarks. If your product is getting 0.3% CTR when the category average is 0.5%, you have a creative or offer problem. If your CVR is 5% when the category average is 12%, you have a conversion problem. If both are near zero and your competitors are also near zero, you have a demand problem.
- **Look for search volume trends, not just static numbers.** A keyword with 2,000 searches per month that was at 5,000 six months ago is a demand problem. A keyword at 2,000 that was at 1,200 six months ago is a growing opportunity. Use historical search volume data from Helium 10 Cerebro or [Kaldon's unmet-demand validation framework](/blog/amazon-discover-unmet-demand-validation-framework) to see direction, not just snapshot volume.

**What a demand problem looks like:**

- Your main keyword has under 500 searches per month
- Top 3 competitor ASINs are selling under 10 units per day combined
- PPC is generating impressions but CTR is under 0.2%
- You launched based on a product idea, not validated search demand

**What to do if you have a demand problem:**

Stop optimizing the listing. You cannot optimize your way into demand. Either pivot the product (new variation, new use case, new price tier) or kill the SKU and move to a product with proven search volume and competitor sales velocity. [Kaldon's unmet-demand playbook](/blog/find-winning-ecommerce-product-unmet-demand-playbook) walks through how to find products where buyers are already searching and competitors are not shipping the solution yet.

If you pass the demand test (search volume above 1,000/month, competitors moving 20+ units/day, category BSR movement healthy), move to Step 2.

## Step 2: Diagnose the Traffic Problem

You confirmed demand exists. Now confirm that traffic is actually reaching your listing. A traffic problem means buyers are searching for your product, but your listing is not appearing in the search results they see.

**How to diagnose traffic:**

- **Run a manual search for your top 5 keywords in incognito mode.** Note where your listing appears. If you are not on page 1 for any of your top 5 keywords, you have a traffic problem.
- **Check your organic rank using a rank tracker.** Helium 10 Keyword Tracker, Jungle Scout Rank Tracker, or similar tools will show you exact rank positions. If you are ranking below position 50 for all primary keywords, organic traffic will be near zero.
- **Check your PPC impression share.** In Amazon Advertising console, look at impression share for your top campaigns. If impression share is under 10%, your bids are too low or your ad relevance score is suppressed. If impression share is above 50% but CTR is under 0.3%, you have a creative problem, not a traffic problem.
- **Verify that your listing is indexed for your target keywords.** Copy your ASIN, then search Amazon for `ASIN + keyword`. If the listing does not appear, that keyword is not indexed. Common indexing failures happen after title edits, backend keyword changes, or when the product does not match the category's required attributes.

**What a traffic problem looks like:**

- Your ASIN is not indexed for primary keywords
- Organic rank is below position 50 for all target terms
- PPC impression share is under 10%
- Competitor listings are appearing on page 1, yours is not

**What to do if you have a traffic problem:**

- **Fix indexing issues.** Re-check that your title, bullets, and backend search terms include the exact keyword phrase. Amazon's indexing logic changed in 2026 to weight "intent coherence" more heavily than keyword density, so make sure the keyword appears in a natural sentence, not stuffed.
- **Increase PPC bids to gain impression share.** If you are bidding $0.50 and the page-1 CPC is $1.20, you will not get traffic. Raise bids to match or exceed category averages until you break onto page 1.
- **Check for title-length compliance.** Amazon cut product title limits to 75 characters for many categories starting July 27, 2026. If your title was optimized at 150+ characters and got truncated, you may have lost keyword indexing and rank. Move overflow keywords into the new searchable "Item Highlights" field or backend search terms.
- **Verify category placement.** If you are in the wrong category or subcategory, Amazon may not surface your listing for relevant searches. Use the Browse Tree Guide to confirm correct placement.

If you pass the traffic test (indexed for primary keywords, ranking on page 1-3 for at least one keyword, PPC impression share above 20%), move to Step 3.

## Step 3: Diagnose the Conversion Problem

You have demand. You have traffic. But the listing still does not convert. This is a conversion mechanics problem: price, offer, creative, trust, or competitive positioning.

**How to diagnose conversion:**

- **Check your CVR in Amazon Advertising and Business Reports.** Category averages range from 8% to 15% depending on product type. If your CVR is below 5%, you have a conversion problem.
- **Compare your price to the top 3 competitors.** If you are more than 15% above the median price and your product does not have a clear differentiator (better reviews, more features, faster shipping), price is likely killing conversion.
- **Check your main image, title, and price in mobile search results.** 70%+ of Amazon traffic is mobile. If your main image does not communicate the product benefit in a 200px thumbnail, or if your title is truncated and unclear, mobile shoppers will scroll past.
- **Read your 1-star and 2-star reviews, and competitor 1-star reviews.** Buyers tell you exactly why they did not buy or why they returned the product. Common conversion killers: size confusion, misleading images, quality below expectations, missing accessories, unclear use case.
- **Check for Buy Box loss or Featured Offer loss.** If you are not winning the Featured Offer, most traffic will convert to a competitor even if they land on your detail page. Amazon's Seller Assistant (launched August 2026) now explains why you lost the Featured Offer. Common reasons: price too high, account health issue, inventory gap, or shipping speed disadvantage.

**What a conversion problem looks like:**

- CVR under 5% when category average is 10%+
- High bounce rate or low detail-page views per session
- Price 20%+ above competitors with no clear differentiator
- Main image does not communicate benefit or use case
- Title truncated on mobile, unclear what the product is
- Zero or few reviews (under 10 reviews is a trust barrier)

**What to do if you have a conversion problem:**

- **Rewrite the listing using buyer language from reviews and Q&A.** Stop using feature-heavy copy. Use the exact phrases buyers use in competitor reviews. For example, if buyers say "fits in small spaces" or "works for apartments," use that exact phrasing in bullets and title. [Kaldon's Create phase](https://kaldon.io/features) generates listing copy by analyzing competitor reviews, Q&A, and search autocomplete data to match buyer language.
- **Fix the main image.** The main image must show the product in use or communicate the core benefit in a single glance. No lifestyle shots allowed on main image per Amazon policy, but the product itself should be clear, large, and benefit-obvious. If you sell a phone mount, show the phone mounted. If you sell a supplement, show the bottle with clear label.
- **Lower the price or add a coupon.** Test a 10% coupon or a 15% price drop for 7 days. If CVR jumps above 8%, you had a price problem. If CVR stays flat, price was not the issue.
- **Add A+ Content and video.** A+ Content increases CVR by an average of 5-10% according to Amazon's own case studies. Video increases CVR even more if it shows the product solving the buyer's problem in under 30 seconds.
- **Run a review-acceleration campaign.** If you have under 15 reviews, conversion will be suppressed. Use Amazon Vine, Request a Review button, or insert cards (if compliant) to hit 15-25 reviews within 30 days.

If you pass the conversion test (CVR above 8%, price competitive, main image clear, winning Featured Offer), but sales are still near zero, move to Step 4.

## Step 4: Check for Platform Suppression

You have demand, traffic, and a listing that should convert, but sales are still near zero or dropped suddenly. This is a suppression problem. Amazon is hiding, throttling, or de-prioritizing your listing.

**How to diagnose suppression:**

- **Check Listing Quality Dashboard in Seller Central.** Amazon will flag title issues, image issues, missing attributes, or non-compliant content. Any red flags here can trigger partial suppression.
- **Check Account Health for policy violations, IP complaints, or restricted-product flags.** A single IP complaint or restricted-product flag can suppress a listing even if it still shows as "Active."
- **Search for your ASIN in incognito mode using the exact product title.** If your listing does not appear on page 1 when you search the exact title, it is suppressed or de-indexed.
- **Check for "Currently Unavailable" status.** In July and August 2026, seller forums reported a spike in "currently unavailable" cases where the ASIN is active in Seller Central but unavailable to buyers. This is often caused by missing compliance fields (FCC ID for electronics, hazmat classification, country of origin) or category-specific attribute gaps.
- **Check for AI-image disclosure compliance.** Starting July 2026, Amazon requires sellers to label AI-generated people in product images and videos. If you used AI-generated lifestyle shots and did not disclose, the listing may be flagged.
- **Check for title-length violations.** Amazon's July 27, 2026 title update cut limits to 75 characters in many categories. If your title is over the limit, Amazon may suppress the listing until you fix it.

**What a suppression problem looks like:**

- ASIN shows "Active" in Seller Central but "Currently Unavailable" on the live site
- Listing does not appear when you search the exact product title
- Red flags in Listing Quality Dashboard
- Policy warnings or restricted-product flags in Account Health
- Sales dropped to zero within 24-48 hours of a catalog edit

**What to do if you have a suppression problem:**

- **Fix all Listing Quality Dashboard errors immediately.** Every red flag is a suppression risk. Common fixes: shorten title to 75 characters, replace non-compliant main image, add missing category attributes (size, color, material, etc.).
- **Resolve Account Health issues.** If you have an IP complaint, respond within 48 hours with a plan of action. If you have a restricted-product flag, remove the violating content or request category approval.
- **Re-index the listing by editing and saving a compliant field.** Sometimes suppression is a stale-index issue. Edit the backend search terms, save, wait 24 hours, and check if the listing re-appears in search.
- **Contact Seller Support with your ASIN and ask for a suppression check.** Support can see internal flags that are not visible in Seller Central. Ask specifically: "Is this ASIN suppressed, throttled, or flagged for any policy or quality issue?"

If you fix suppression and the listing still does not sell, loop back to Step 1. You may have misdiagnosed demand.

## Common Diagnostic Mistakes (and How to Avoid Them)

Most sellers waste weeks on the wrong fix because they skip the demand test or misinterpret traffic data. Here are the most common mistakes:

**Mistake 1: Assuming traffic = demand.** PPC impressions do not prove demand. Impressions mean your ad showed up. Demand means buyers clicked, added to cart, and purchased. If you have 10,000 impressions and 5 clicks, you do not have a demand problem. You have a creative problem (bad image or title) or a targeting problem (wrong keywords).

**Mistake 2: Optimizing the listing before confirming traffic.** If you are not indexed for your primary keywords, rewriting bullets will not help. Fix indexing first, then optimize.

**Mistake 3: Blaming Amazon's algorithm when the product has no search volume.** If your primary keyword has 200 searches per month, you do not have an algorithm problem. You have a product-market fit problem. Kill the SKU or pivot.

**Mistake 4: Running PPC without checking organic rank.** PPC accelerates what is already working. If your organic rank is below position 100 for all primary keywords, PPC will be expensive and low-converting. Fix organic rank first by improving listing quality, getting reviews, and lowering price temporarily.

**Mistake 5: Editing the listing too frequently.** Every title or image edit can trigger a re-index delay or temporary rank drop. Make one change, wait 48-72 hours, measure, then make the next change. Batch edits cause cascading suppression issues.

## When to Kill the Product vs. Fix the Listing

Sometimes the correct answer is not to optimize. It is to kill the SKU and move to a better product. Here is when to kill vs. fix:

**Kill the product if:**

- Primary keyword search volume is under 500/month and declining
- Top 3 competitors are selling under 10 units/day combined
- You have run traffic, conversion, and suppression fixes for 60+ days and CVR is still under 3%
- The product requires active demand creation (education, influencer push, paid social) to generate sales

**Fix the listing if:**

- Search volume is above 1,000/month and stable or growing
- Competitors are selling 20+ units/day
- You have identified a specific fixable issue (indexing, price, image, suppression)
- CVR is above 5% but traffic is low (fixable with PPC or SEO)

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my Amazon listing not getting any sales even though it is live?

You likely have one of four problems: no demand (the market does not want this product), no traffic (buyers cannot find your listing), poor conversion (traffic arrives but does not buy), or platform suppression (Amazon is hiding or throttling the listing). Run the demand test first by checking search volume and competitor sales velocity. If demand exists, check indexing and organic rank. If traffic exists, check CVR and price. If all three pass, check for suppression flags in Listing Quality Dashboard and Account Health.

### How do I know if my Amazon listing has a demand problem or an optimization problem?

Check search volume for your primary keywords and sales velocity for your top 3 competitors. If your main keyword has under 500 searches per month and competitors are selling under 10 units per day, you have a demand problem and optimization will not fix it. If search volume is above 1,000 per month and competitors are moving 20+ units per day, you have traffic, conversion, or suppression issues that are fixable.

### What does it mean if my Amazon listing is live but shows as currently unavailable?

This is a suppression or compliance issue, not a demand or optimization problem. Check Listing Quality Dashboard for title-length violations, missing category attributes, or non-compliant images. Check Account Health for policy warnings or restricted-product flags. Common causes in 2026 include title over 75 characters, missing FCC ID for electronics, or AI-generated images without disclosure. Fix all red flags, re-save the listing, and wait 24-48 hours for re-indexing.

### How long should I wait before deciding my Amazon listing will not sell?

If you validated demand before launch (search volume above 1,000/month, competitors selling 20+ units/day), give the listing 30 days with active PPC and organic rank on page 1-3. If after 30 days your CVR is still under 3% and you have fixed traffic, conversion, and suppression issues, the product likely has a product-market fit problem. If you did not validate demand before launch, run the demand test immediately and kill the SKU if search volume is under 500/month or competitors are not converting.
