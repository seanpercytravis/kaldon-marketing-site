## Pull the Amazon Sponsored Products Search Term Report Every 14 Days

The Amazon Sponsored Products search term report shows the actual queries shoppers typed before clicking your ad. Navigate to Campaign Manager, select Sponsored Products, click the "Search term" tab, set a 14- or 30-day date range, and export the CSV. Most sellers wait too long or never export at all, bleeding budget on terms they would immediately recognize as irrelevant if they simply looked.

Set a calendar reminder for every 14 days during launch or high-spend periods, and monthly once campaigns stabilize. Frequency matters: a term with 5 clicks and zero orders on day 7 may hit 15 clicks and zero orders by day 14, crossing the threshold for negative exact treatment. If you wait 60 or 90 days, you have already paid for 40+ wasted clicks.

In the exported CSV, sort by clicks descending, then filter for rows with zero orders. Flag any search term with 10 or more clicks and zero conversions as a negative exact candidate. Some operators use 15–20 clicks as the threshold to avoid premature exclusions on low-traffic SKUs. The specific number is less important than consistency: pick a rule, document it, and apply it every cycle. High spend and zero orders is the clearest signal of wasted money, and those rows become your negative keyword input list.

## Flag High-Spend, Zero-Order Terms and Add Them as Negative Exact

Once you have flagged the wasted-spend rows, copy the exact search term strings and add them to your campaign or ad group as **negative exact** keywords. Negative exact blocks only that precise query, leaving close variants and broader themes untouched. This surgical approach prevents accidental over-negation that can kill discovery traffic.

For example, if "stainless steel water bottle mini" drove 12 clicks and zero orders, add it as negative exact. If three different "mini" queries all waste spend ("mini water bottle", "water bottle mini size", "compact mini bottle"), consider a **negative phrase** for "mini" to block all queries containing that modifier. Negative phrase has broader reach but higher risk: a single-word negative phrase can exclude profitable long-tail terms you have not seen yet, so use it only for recurring irrelevant themes (wrong materials, wrong use cases, competitor brand names, non-purchase intent like "free" or "how to").

Inside Amazon Campaign Manager, navigate to the campaign or ad group, click the "Negative keywords" tab, and click "Add negative keywords." Paste your list, select "Negative exact" or "Negative phrase" match type, and save. You can also upload via bulk operations if you are managing hundreds of negatives across dozens of campaigns. Amazon's bulk template for negative keywords is available in the Campaign Manager under "Bulk operations" and [documented in Amazon Advertising Help](https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GPVTCZRJ7G9HXHWB). Most agencies and experienced operators prefer bulk uploads because they allow version control, audit trails, and faster cross-campaign updates.

## Avoid Over-Negation: The Stale Negative Problem and How to Audit It

Negative keywords are not permanent. Shopper behavior shifts. New competitors enter. Seasonal trends change. A query that wasted spend in February may convert profitably in July. A term you blocked as irrelevant for SKU A may be highly relevant for SKU B when you launch it six months later. Stale negatives become silent growth caps: they block traffic you no longer want to block, and you will not notice unless you audit.

Every 90 days, export your full negative keyword list across all campaigns and ad groups. Review the list with fresh eyes. Ask: Is this term still irrelevant? Did we launch a new SKU that makes this term relevant again? Has the competitive landscape changed? If the answer is yes, remove the negative keyword and let the term back into your campaigns under close watch. Track it in the next search term report cycle. If it still wastes spend, re-negate it. If it converts, you just unlocked hidden growth.

Documentation is critical. When you add a negative keyword, record the date, the campaign or ad group, the reason ("15 clicks, 0 orders, 60-day window" or "competitor brand name"), and the person who made the change. A simple shared spreadsheet or a column in your bulk upload file works. Without this record, you will forget why you blocked a term, and you will be afraid to remove it later. Operators who skip documentation end up with 500+ negative keywords and no idea which ones are still useful.

## When to Use Negative Exact vs. Negative Phrase: Match Type Decision Tree

Negative exact blocks only the specific search term you enter, character for character. "Stainless steel water bottle 64 oz" as negative exact will block that query but allow "stainless steel water bottle 64 ounce" or "64 oz stainless water bottle." Use negative exact for surgical removal of individual wasted queries with enough spend history (10–20 clicks, zero orders) to justify exclusion.

Negative phrase blocks any search query that contains the phrase you enter, in the same word order. "Mini water bottle" as negative phrase will block "mini water bottle," "stainless steel mini water bottle," "best mini water bottle," and any other query with "mini water bottle" in sequence. Use negative phrase for recurring irrelevant themes: wrong materials ("plastic" when you only sell metal), wrong sizes ("mini" or "giant" when you sell standard), wrong use cases ("replacement lid" when you sell complete bottles), non-purchase intent ("free," "coupon," "review," "how to"), and competitor brand names.

The decision tree: if a single query wasted 10+ clicks, use negative exact. If 3+ different queries share the same irrelevant modifier or theme, use negative phrase for the shared word or phrase. Never use a single-word negative phrase unless you are certain no profitable query could contain that word. For example, "kids" as negative phrase will block "kids water bottle," "water bottle for kids," "best kids bottle," and potentially "adult and kids bottle set." If you sell family packs or bundles, the last query might convert. A safer approach: review the actual search terms and negative exact the specific "kids" queries that wasted spend, rather than blanket-blocking all "kids" traffic.

## FAQ: Amazon Sponsored Products Negative Keywords (July 2026)

**What is the best threshold for adding a negative keyword in Amazon Sponsored Products?**

Most operators use 10–20 clicks and zero orders as the threshold for negative exact. If a search term drives 10+ clicks with no conversions, it is wasting budget and should be blocked. Some sellers prefer 15 or 20 clicks to avoid premature exclusions on low-traffic SKUs. Pick a number, document it, and apply it consistently every 14 or 30 days when you pull search term reports.

**Should I use negative exact or negative phrase for Amazon Sponsored Products?**

Use negative exact for individual wasted queries (one term with 10+ clicks and zero orders). Use negative phrase for recurring irrelevant themes: wrong materials, wrong sizes, competitor brands, or non-purchase intent words like "free," "cheap," or "how to." Negative phrase has broader reach and higher risk of blocking profitable long-tail terms, so apply it only when 3+ different queries share the same irrelevant modifier.

**How often should I update negative keywords in Amazon Sponsored Products campaigns?**

Every 14 days during product launch or high-spend periods, and monthly once campaigns stabilize. Set a calendar reminder. Pull the search term report, flag high-spend/zero-order terms, add negatives, and document the changes. Operators who skip this cadence waste 15–40% of their Sponsored Products budget on irrelevant clicks.

**Can I use the same negative keyword list for Amazon and Walmart Sponsored Products?**

Yes. Walmart launched negative keyword targeting for Sponsored Products on July 29, 2026, with the same match types (exact and phrase) as Amazon. Export search term reports from both platforms, apply the same thresholds (10–20 clicks, zero orders = negative exact), and maintain a shared negative keyword list. Cross-platform consistency simplifies reporting and governance, and cross-platform waste signals often reveal stronger unmet demand opportunities.

**How do I avoid blocking profitable keywords by accident with negative keywords?**

Document every negative keyword with the date, reason, and campaign. Review your full negative keyword list every 90 days and remove any terms that are no longer irrelevant (new SKU launch, seasonal shift, or competitive change). Use negative exact instead of negative phrase when possible, because exact match blocks only the specific query and leaves close variants open. Never use single-word negative phrase unless you are certain no profitable query could contain that word.
