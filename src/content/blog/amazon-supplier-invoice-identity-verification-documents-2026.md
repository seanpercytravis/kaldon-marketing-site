## What Amazon Actually Checks During Supplier Invoice Identity Verification

Amazon now verifies supplier identity through cross-document consistency, not invoice formatting alone. Accounts reinstated in November 2026 submitted: supplier-issued PDF invoices matching bank wire receipts and FBA delivery records, supplier company-registry records, business websites, order histories, brand authorization letters, and GTIN ownership proof. Generic rejections typically mean Amazon could not independently verify the supplier exists, the invoice matches completed payments and inventory, or you are authorized to sell the brand. The required date window is 180 days for most identity and Section 3 reviews, 365 days for policy appeals.

Between October 21 and November 6, 2026, UK and European sellers reported submitting wholesale invoices, bank statements, supplier contact information, Companies House records, and authenticity declarations, yet still receiving **"supplier information could not be verified"** rejections without actionable feedback. One deactivated seller offered to have the supplier verify invoices directly. Amazon did not accept the offer and instead requested invoices from a different supplier dated within 180 days.

The enforcement shift: Amazon is no longer accepting invoice OCR or format compliance as proof of supplier identity. Instead, the platform is building an identity graph connecting the legal entity, payment account, inventory movement, brand authorization, and upstream supplier.

## The Exact Document Combination That Passes Review

Based on November 2026 reinstatement cases and Amazon Seller Forum discussions, the complete evidence package includes:

### 1. Supplier-Issued Invoice (PDF or Image)

Required fields:

- Supplier legal business name and full registered address
- Supplier company logo (recommended)
- Your legal business name and registered address (exact match to Seller Central)
- Invoice number and issue date within 180 days (identity/Section 3) or 365 days (policy appeals)
- Product descriptions matching your ASIN titles
- UPC or EAN codes for every unit
- Quantities matching your FBA shipment records
- Unit prices and total amount paid
- Payment terms (wire, check, credit card)
- Supplier contact: phone, email, website

**Rejected formats:** screenshots, `.xlsx`, `.docx`, pro forma invoices, self-issued invoices, invoices from retailers or unauthorized resellers, invoices sent via email attachment (must upload through Account Health workflow).

### 2. Payment Records Matching Invoice Total and Date

Acceptable proof:

- Bank wire transfer receipt showing exact invoice total, supplier name, date
- Credit card statement line item (business card preferred)
- Canceled check image (front and back)
- Payment platform record (Alibaba Trade Assurance, PayPal business)

**Critical detail:** Payment sender name and bank account must match your registered Seller Central legal entity and bank-on-file. Payment recipient must match supplier invoice legal name.

### 3. Delivery or Inventory Records Matching Invoice Quantities

Acceptable proof:

- FBA shipment creation record showing FNSKU, ASIN, quantity received
- Freight forwarder bill of lading or commercial invoice
- Warehouse receipt (if using 3PL before FBA)
- Supplier packing list or delivery confirmation

**Match required:** ASIN quantities on invoice = quantities shipped to FBA within 30 days of invoice date.

### 4. Supplier Company Verification

Amazon is independently checking whether the supplier exists and operates as a legitimate manufacturer or authorized distributor. Provide:

- **Company registry record:** China National Enterprise Credit Information, Dun & Bradstreet, Companies House (UK), Secretary of State business search (US)
- **Business website:** Working URL showing products, contact information, company history
- **Order history:** Email thread, purchase order, proforma invoice, signed contract
- **Supplier contact confirmation:** Letter on company letterhead confirming transaction, signed and stamped

If the supplier has no independent web presence, no registry record, or operates only through Alibaba storefronts, Amazon may classify them as a reseller rather than a manufacturer or tier-one distributor.

### 5. Brand Authorization (If Selling Established Brands)

Required when your invoice comes from a distributor rather than the brand owner:

- **Letter of authorization** from the brand owner or trademark holder, addressed to your legal business name, granting permission to sell on Amazon
- **Proof of brand relationship:** GS1 Company Prefix ownership, Brand Registry enrollment, trademark certificate, direct manufacturer agreement
- **Product authenticity proof:** Photos of physical products showing UPC label, brand logo, packaging

Sellers report that distributor invoices alone no longer establish brand permission. If the brand did not authorize you to sell on Amazon, the invoice may prove you bought the product but not that you are allowed to list it.

## Why Amazon Rejects Complete Invoice Packages: The Four Hidden Signals

### Signal 1: Supplier Cannot Be Independently Verified

Amazon appears to cross-check supplier business names against company registries, business databases, and web presence. If the supplier name on your invoice does not match a registered legal entity with a working website and contact information, the invoice is rejected even if formatting is correct.

**Fix:** Before ordering, verify the supplier's legal registration in their home country. Use [China supplier verification steps](/blog/verify-china-suppliers-before-deposit-amazon-invoice-checklist) for Alibaba or 1688 orders. For US distributors, check Secretary of State business records. For UK suppliers, verify on Companies House.

### Signal 2: Payment, Invoice, and Seller Identity Do Not Match

Amazon checks whether the business name on the invoice, the payment sender, and your registered Seller Central entity are identical. Mismatches trigger rejection:

- Invoice addressed to "ABC Trading LLC" but payment sent from personal checking account
- Invoice from "XYZ Manufacturing Co., Ltd." but payment sent to "XYZ Sales Agent"
- Seller Central registered as "John Doe DBA Doe Retail" but invoice addressed to "John Doe"

**Fix:** Ensure invoice legal name, payment account legal name, and Seller Central legal entity match exactly. If you changed your business structure mid-year, update Seller Central before submitting invoices.

### Signal 3: Inventory Movement Does Not Match Invoice

Amazon cross-references invoice quantities, dates, and ASINs against FBA shipment records. Red flags:

- Invoice dated June 1, FBA shipment created October 15 (90+ day gap suggests resale or dropshipping)
- Invoice shows 500 units, FBA received 50 units (incomplete shipment or commingled inventory)
- Invoice lists Product A, but you shipped Product B under the same ASIN

**Fix:** Upload invoices within 30 days of the corresponding FBA shipment. Match ASIN, quantity, and product description exactly. If you split one purchase order across multiple shipments, upload the full invoice plus a reconciliation sheet.

### Signal 4: Supplier Is Not Authorized to Sell the Brand

Even if the supplier is a legitimate distributor, Amazon may reject the invoice if the supplier is not an authorized channel partner for that brand. Current enforcement appears to distinguish:

- **Manufacturer or brand owner:** Accepted without additional authorization
- **Authorized tier-one distributor:** Accepted if you provide distributor agreement or brand letter
- **Secondary distributor or reseller:** Rejected unless you provide brand authorization letter

**Fix:** Contact the brand directly to verify authorized distributors before purchasing. Request a letter of authorization addressed to your business name. For private-label products, ensure you own the trademark or brand name.

## The Misrouted Invoice Request Problem: When Amazon Asks for Invoices in the Wrong Workflow

Between October and November 2026, European sellers reported receiving invoice requests inside the **identity verification workflow** rather than the **product authenticity or restricted-product workflow**. The identity-verification upload interface in some regions does not include a clearly labeled "product invoice" field, causing sellers to upload invoices as "proof of address" or "business registration" documents.

When this happens:

1. Amazon's review team may not see the invoice at all, or may classify it as the wrong document type
2. The case is rejected with generic language like "documents do not meet requirements"
3. Resubmitting the same invoice through the same workflow produces the same rejection

**Fix:** If you receive an invoice request through identity verification, reply to the case stating: "I am uploading a supplier invoice as requested. This invoice is for [ASIN/product name], issued by [supplier legal name], dated [date], total [amount]. I am also providing payment receipt, company registry record, and delivery confirmation." This written explanation helps the review team classify the document correctly.

If the rejection repeats, escalate through Seller Support with case ID, invoice number, and a request to route the case to the **restricted product** or **product authenticity team** rather than the identity-verification team.

## How to Diagnose a Generic Rejection: The Four-Step Audit

When Amazon replies "supplier information could not be verified" without specifics, run this audit:

### Step 1: Verify Supplier Legal Entity Match

Google the supplier business name from the invoice. Confirm:

- Registry record in home country (China: qcc.com or gsxt.gov.cn; US: state Secretary of State; UK: Companies House)
- Working business website with contact information
- Address on invoice matches registry address

If no registry record or website exists, Amazon likely classified the supplier as unverifiable.

### Step 2: Check Payment-Sender Identity Match

Confirm:

- Payment account legal name = Seller Central legal name (exact match, no DBA variants)
- Payment recipient legal name = supplier invoice legal name
- Payment date within 10 days of invoice date
- Payment amount = invoice total (or invoice total minus deposit if paying balance)

### Step 3: Reconcile Invoice Against FBA Records

Open FBA shipment detail. Confirm:

- Shipment created within 30 days of invoice date
- ASIN quantities match invoice line items
- Product titles match invoice descriptions
- UPC/EAN codes on invoice match ASIN UPC in catalog

### Step 4: Check Brand Authorization

If you are selling an established brand:

- Is the supplier the brand owner, or a distributor?
- If distributor: Do you have written authorization from the brand to sell on Amazon?
- If no authorization: Contact the brand and request a letter, or switch to a different product

If all four steps pass and Amazon still rejects the invoice, the issue is likely document format, file type, or workflow routing.

## Escalation Paths When Standard Appeals Fail

When repeated submissions through Account Health do not resolve the case:

### Path 1: Request Human Review and Specific Rejection Reason

Reply to the rejection case:

> "I have submitted: (1) supplier-issued PDF invoice #[number] dated [date], (2) bank wire receipt matching invoice total, (3) FBA shipment record showing matching quantities, (4) supplier company registry record, (5) brand authorization letter. Please confirm which specific document or field failed review so I can provide corrected evidence."

This forces the review team to either identify the gap or escalate internally.

### Path 2: Offer Direct Supplier Verification

If the supplier is willing to confirm the transaction directly, reply:

> "My supplier [legal name] is available to verify this invoice directly with Amazon. Please provide contact instructions or a verification form. Supplier contact: [email], [phone]."

Note: November 2026 cases indicate Amazon does not always accept this offer, but it demonstrates good faith and may move the case to a specialized team.

### Path 3: Escalate Through Seller Performance Email

If Account Health cases loop without resolution, email `seller-performance@amazon.com` (US), `seller-performance@amazon.co.uk` (UK), or the relevant marketplace address with:

- Case ID from Account Health
- ASIN or account suspension reason
- Summary: "I have submitted [list documents]. All documents match Amazon's published requirements. I request human review and specific rejection reason."
- Attach all documents as PDFs in one email

### Path 4: Request New Supplier Invoice (If Timeline Permits)

If the original supplier is unresponsive, untraceable, or the invoice is older than 180 days, source the same product from a tier-one authorized distributor and request a new invoice dated within the current review window. This resets the verification process with stronger supplier evidence.

## Date Windows by Enforcement Type: 180 Days vs. 365 Days

Amazon applies different invoice recency rules depending on the enforcement trigger:

| Enforcement Type | Invoice Date Window | Common Triggers |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity Verification (Section 3)** | 180 days | Account registration, business-structure change, bank-account change, ownership change, address change |
| **Restricted Category Approval** | 180 days | Gated brands, topical products, hazmat, grocery, automotive |
| **Product Authenticity Review** | 180 days (recommended) | Customer complaints, inauthentic-product claim, rights-owner report |
| **Policy Violation Appeal** | 365 days | Used-sold-as-new, condition complaint, ASIN-level suspension |

November 2026 cases confirm that Amazon sometimes requests 180-day invoices even when the violation type historically accepted 365-day invoices. When in doubt, provide the most recent invoice available and explain the purchase timeline in your appeal.

If your most recent qualifying invoice is older than the required window, you have three options:

1. **Purchase additional inventory from the same supplier** and request a new invoice, then resubmit
2. **Switch to a different product** with a recent invoice and close the flagged listing
3. **Appeal with historical invoice plus payment and delivery records** and explain the business timeline (lower success rate but sometimes accepted)

## Document Format and Submission Rules That Trigger Automatic Rejection

Amazon's OCR and authenticity checks automatically reject:

- **Screenshots** of invoices, emails, PDFs, or websites
- **Editable file formats:** `.xlsx`, `.docx`, `.txt`, `.csv`
- **Invoices sent via email attachment** rather than uploaded through Account Health
- **Pro forma invoices** (quotations, not completed transactions)
- **Self-issued invoices** (you cannot invoice yourself)
- **Invoices with digital alterations:** Photoshop edits, whited-out fields, pasted logos, font mismatches
- **Invoices from retail stores** unless you are approved for retail arbitrage and the receipt shows business tax exemption

Accepted formats:

- **PDF** generated by supplier's accounting system
- **JPEG or PNG** photo of paper invoice (clear, full page, no shadows or reflections)
- **Uploaded directly through Account Health case** or the verification workflow interface

If your supplier sends invoices as Excel files, request a PDF export or print-to-PDF version on company letterhead before uploading.

## What to Do When You Cannot Get a Compliant Invoice

If your supplier cannot or will not provide an invoice meeting Amazon's requirements:

### Option 1: Request a Corrected Invoice

Email the supplier:

> "I need an updated invoice for Amazon compliance. Please include: (1) your legal business name and registered address, (2) my legal business name [your name], (3) product descriptions, (4) UPC codes, (5) quantities, (6) unit prices, (7) invoice number and date, (8) your company logo and contact information. Please send as PDF on company letterhead."

Most B2B suppliers will issue a corrected invoice within 24 to 48 hours.

### Option 2: Compile Supporting Evidence

If the supplier is unresponsive but the transaction was legitimate, compile:

- Original invoice (even if incomplete)
- Proforma invoice or purchase order
- Email thread confirming order and payment
- Payment receipt
- Delivery tracking or bill of lading
- Supplier website screenshot showing products and contact info
- Company registry record

Upload all documents with a cover letter explaining: "Supplier [name] is no longer responsive. I am providing all available transaction records to verify this purchase."

Success rate is lower, but some cases are approved when the evidence package is complete and consistent.

### Option 3: Switch Suppliers for Future Inventory

If the supplier cannot be verified, source the same product from a tier-one authorized distributor and request a compliant invoice before shipping. Close the flagged ASIN and relaunch under a new listing with the new supplier's invoice.
