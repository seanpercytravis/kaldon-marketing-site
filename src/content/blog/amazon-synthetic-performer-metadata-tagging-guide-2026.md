## What is Amazon's synthetic performer metadata rule?

Amazon now requires that any product image, A+ content module, or product video showing a **photorealistic AI-generated person** carry the IPTC/XMP keyword `contains-synthetic-performer` in the `dc:subject` metadata field at the file level. The rule went live on July 27, 2026, and enforcement began on August 15, 2026. This is not a visible watermark. The tag lives in the file metadata and Amazon's systems read it during upload to determine whether to show a disclosure indicator to shoppers.

The policy applies globally. It covers main images, secondary images, lifestyle shots, A+ content, brand stores, and product videos. It does not apply to images of real people (even if you used AI to remove a background or adjust lighting), non-photorealistic illustrations, or images without people. It only applies when you generate a realistic human who does not exist.

Amazon ties this rule to three recent laws: New York's Synthetic Performer Disclosure Law (operative June 2026), the EU Artificial Intelligence Act (rollout August 2, 2026), and California's AI Transparency Act (operative August 2, 2026). All three require disclosure when ads or product materials depict synthetic humans. Amazon operationalizes these frameworks with a single metadata schema sellers can apply at scale.

## How to add the `contains-synthetic-performer` tag to your images

You write the tag into IPTC/XMP metadata using an editor that supports the Dublin Core subject field. The tag must be present in the file you upload to Amazon, not just in your working file. Here is the step-by-step workflow.

### Step 1: Export all active ASIN images and classify them

Pull every image and video for your active listings. Sort them into three buckets:

1. Images with human figures.
2. Images where those humans are AI-generated (fully synthetic, not real people edited with AI tools).
3. Images where you still have the source file with editable metadata.

If you used a tool like Midjourney, DALL-E, or Stable Diffusion to create a model or customer in a lifestyle scene, that image needs the tag. If you photographed a real person and used Photoshop's generative fill to clean the background, that image does not need the tag. If you used an AI tool to create a product render with no people, no tag is required.

### Step 2: Choose your IPTC/XMP editor

You need software that can batch-edit IPTC metadata. The most common tools are:

- **Adobe Photoshop**: Open the image, go to File → File Info, select the IPTC tab, find the Keywords field (which maps to `dc:subject`), add `contains-synthetic-performer`, and save.
- **Adobe Bridge**: Select multiple images, open File Info, add the keyword in the IPTC tab, and apply to all selected files.
- **Adobe Lightroom**: Use the Keyword panel to add `contains-synthetic-performer` to the selected images, then export.
- **ExifTool** (command line): Run `exiftool -IPTC:Keywords+=contains-synthetic-performer image.jpg` to append the keyword. Use a shell script to batch-process folders.
- **PixPix** (Windows/Mac GUI): Open the image, navigate to the IPTC section, add the keyword, and save.

For large catalogs (hundreds or thousands of images), ExifTool or Adobe Bridge are the fastest. ExifTool can process entire directories in seconds and log each change for audit trails.

### Step 3: Write the keyword to `dc:subject` and verify

The IPTC Keywords field in most editors corresponds to the XMP `dc:subject` array. Add `contains-synthetic-performer` exactly as written (lowercase, hyphenated, no spaces). Do not add extra keywords like "AI-generated" or "synthetic" unless you want them for internal DAM purposes. Amazon looks for the exact string.

After tagging, verify the metadata using ExifTool or Bridge. Open the file and confirm the keyword appears in the IPTC section. If you export from Lightroom or Photoshop, re-check the exported file. Some workflows strip metadata during export if settings are misconfigured.

### Step 4: Upload the tagged file to Amazon

Upload the image through Seller Central, Manage Inventory, or bulk upload templates. Amazon's system scans the file metadata on upload. If the tag is present and correct, the image is accepted and a disclosure indicator is added to the listing for shoppers. If the tag is missing on an image that Amazon's AI detection flags as containing a synthetic person, the listing can be suppressed.

### Step 5: Store compliance records

Keep a log of which ASINs received tagged images, the date of upload, and a copy of the tagged source file. If Amazon challenges a listing, you need proof that you disclosed properly. Export the IPTC data from your DAM or use ExifTool to generate a CSV report of all files with `contains-synthetic-performer` in their metadata.

## When you do NOT need the synthetic performer tag

Amazon's guidance is narrow. You only tag photorealistic AI-generated humans. You do not tag:

- Images of real people, even if you used AI to edit exposure, remove blemishes, replace backgrounds, or adjust colors.
- Non-photorealistic characters from films, TV shows, video games, or illustrated art.
- Product-only images with no people (even if the product itself was AI-generated).
- Images where the AI tool generated props, backgrounds, or abstract elements but no human figures.
- Hands, arms, or partial body shots of real people, even if AI-retouched.

If you are unsure whether your image depicts a "photorealistic AI-generated person," ask: could a shopper mistake this for a photo of a real human? If yes and the human is fully synthetic, tag it. If the base image is a photograph of a real person and you only used AI for cleanup or composition, do not tag it.

Multiple seller education posts from August 2026 stress this distinction because early confusion led to over-tagging. Over-tagging is not a violation, but it adds unnecessary disclosure indicators that may reduce conversion. Under-tagging is the compliance risk.

## Broader context: main image compliance beyond synthetic performers

The synthetic performer tag is one piece of Amazon's August 2026 enforcement wave. Sellers are also being hit with suppression for main image violations that have nothing to do with AI. The most common patterns are:

1. **Non-white backgrounds**: Main images must have a pure white background (RGB 255, 255, 255) in most categories. AI-generated images with off-white, gradient, or textured backgrounds are being flagged.
2. **Product visibility under 85 percent**: Main images must show the product filling at least 85 percent of the frame. Lifestyle shots with excessive white space or context are being suppressed.
3. **Composite images**: Combining multiple product angles or views in a single main image is prohibited in most categories. This rule applies whether the composite is AI-generated or manually assembled in Photoshop.
4. **Text, logos, or graphics**: Main images cannot include promotional text, brand logos (except when embossed on the product itself), or graphic overlays. AI tools that add "Sale" or "New" badges are generating violations.

These rules apply to all listings, not just those with AI-generated people.
