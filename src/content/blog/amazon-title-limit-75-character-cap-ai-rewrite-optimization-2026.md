## TLDR

Amazon's 75-character title limit takes effect July 27, 2026, replacing the previous ~200-character cap for most categories. Titles exceeding 75 characters will be automatically rewritten by Amazon AI. You now have 75 characters for the title plus 125 searchable characters in the new Item Highlights field (200 total). Brand-registered sellers get a 14-day review window for AI changes; non-brand sellers do not. Prioritize top-revenue ASINs first to avoid conversion drops during the transition.

## What Changed and Why It Matters for Your Catalog

Amazon moved from an informal 130- to 200-character title guideline to a hard 75-character limit (including spaces) for all non-media categories starting July 27, 2026. Media (books, music, video) remains exempt. The change is part of Amazon's push for mobile-first display and consistency across marketplaces.

If your titles exceed 75 characters after July 27, Amazon's AI will gradually rewrite them. Brand-registered sellers receive AI-generated recommendations through the Review Listings Changes tool and have 14 days to approve, modify, or reject. Non-brand-registered sellers do not get advance notice and titles are rewritten without review.

The 125-character Item Highlights field is now live and searchable. This is where you move material details, use cases, and secondary keywords that no longer fit in the title. Total searchable character count is still 200 (75 title + 125 highlights), but the split changes how you structure messaging and prioritize keywords.

According to recent seller forum threads with 107+ responses, the top operational concern is timing: many sellers are rebuilding catalogs during Prime Day prep, which raises conversion risk if you change titles on high-velocity ASINs without testing.

## How to Decide What Stays in the Title vs. Moves to Item Highlights

Title (75 characters): **Brand name, product type, and one primary differentiator.** This is what appears in mobile search results and drives click-through. Lead with the most search-weighted keyword phrase that also reads naturally.

**Example (60 characters):**
BrightLux LED Desk Lamp, Dimmable, USB-C Rechargeable

Item Highlights (125 characters): **Materials, dimensions, use cases, secondary features, and long-tail keywords.** This field is searchable and displays on the detail page alongside the title. It absorbs the feature list that used to bloat titles.

**Example (118 characters):**
Aluminum body, 3 color temps (3000K/4000K/5000K), 10-hour battery, touch controls. Ideal for home office, study, and bedside.

**Decision framework:**

1. Run a search term report for each ASIN. Identify the top 3 keywords by order revenue.
2. The highest-volume keyword phrase goes in the title, formatted as [Brand] [Keyword Phrase] [One Unique Feature].
3. Secondary keywords, materials, and use cases move to Item Highlights.
4. Tertiary keywords and synonyms go into bullet points and backend search terms.

Do **not** waste title characters on promotional language ("Best Seller," "#1 Rated") or redundant phrasing ("High Quality"). Amazon's AI recommendations will strip these automatically.

## How to Audit Your Catalog and Prioritize SKUs by Revenue Impact

Manual title rewrites across thousands of ASINs is impractical. Triage by revenue to protect conversion on your top sellers first.

**Step-by-step audit workflow:**

1. **Export catalog data from Seller Central.** Include: ASIN, SKU, current title, title character count, last 30-day revenue, last 30-day sessions.
2. **Flag all titles over 75 characters.** Sort by revenue descending.
3. **Segment into three priority tiers:**
   - **Tier 1 (top 20% of revenue):** Rewrite manually before July 27. Test on a small batch first to measure CTR and conversion impact.
   - **Tier 2 (next 30%):** Use AI-generated recommendations if you are brand-registered, then manually review and approve within the 14-day window.
   - **Tier 3 (bottom 50%):** Accept Amazon AI rewrites or batch-edit using a template if you have capacity.
4. **For brand-registered sellers:** Enable Review Listings Changes notifications in Seller Central under Settings > Notification Preferences. You will receive alerts when Amazon generates AI title recommendations.
5. **For non-brand sellers:** You do not get advance review. Rewrite proactively before July 27 to avoid uncontrolled AI edits.

Multiple agency operators recommend completing Tier 1 rewrites by July 20 to allow a 7-day stabilization window before enforcement begins. This avoids triggering rank volatility during the first wave of AI rewrites.

## How to Write a 75-Character Title That Maintains Ranking and Conversion

The constraint forces clarity. A well-structured 75-character title outperforms a keyword-stuffed 180-character title on mobile CTR.

**Formula:**

[Brand Name] [Product Type] [Primary Keyword Phrase] [One Differentiator]

**Character budget allocation:**

- Brand name: 8–15 characters (use acronym or short form if possible)
- Product type: 10–20 characters ("LED Desk Lamp," "Protein Powder," "Yoga Mat")
- Primary keyword phrase: 15–25 characters (include the highest-volume search term)
- Differentiator: 10–20 characters (material, unique feature, or use case)
- Commas and spaces: 5–10 characters

**Good examples:**

- **Anker PowerCore 20K, USB-C Fast Charge, 65W PD Battery Pack** (66 chars)
- **Lululemon Align Leggings, High-Rise, Nulu Fabric, 25-Inch** (64 chars)
- **BodyArmor Sports Drink, Mixed Berry, 12-Pack, Low Sugar** (61 chars)

**Bad examples (over 75 or weak structure):**

- **Best Selling Premium Quality Stainless Steel Insulated Water Bottle with Straw for Sports, Gym, Hiking, Travel, BPA-Free, 32oz** (128 chars, keyword stuffing, no brand)
- **Yoga Mat** (8 chars, under-optimized, no differentiation)

**Mobile display test:** Before finalizing, preview the title on a mobile device. Amazon truncates at ~60 characters in mobile search results. Your brand name and primary keyword must appear in the first 60 characters.

## How to Use Item Highlights Without Diluting Keyword Coverage

Item Highlights is searchable and indexed. Treat it as an extension of your title, not a secondary bullet list.

**What to include:**

- **Materials and construction:** "Aluminum alloy frame," "100% organic cotton," "BPA-free Tritan plastic"
- **Dimensions and weight:** "10 x 8 x 2 inches, weighs 1.2 lbs"
- **Use cases and scenarios:** "Ideal for home office, dorm rooms, and travel"
- **Secondary features:** "Touch controls, 3 brightness levels, auto shut-off timer"
- **Long-tail keywords:** "Compatible with MacBook Pro, Dell XPS, HP Spectre"

**What to exclude:**

- Duplicate information already in the title
- Promotional claims ("#1 Best Seller," "Limited Time Offer")
- Overly technical jargon unless your audience is B2B or prosumer

**Example Item Highlights (122 characters):**

Stainless steel exterior, vacuum insulation keeps drinks cold 24h or hot 12h. Fits car cup holders. Leak-proof flip lid. Dishwasher safe.

**Keyword coverage strategy:**

If your product serves multiple use cases (e.g., a water bottle used for gym, hiking, and office), include one use case in the title and the other two in Item Highlights. This maintains broad keyword coverage without exceeding the 75-character title cap.

## How to Prevent AI Rewrite Damage to Brand Positioning

Amazon's AI title recommendations optimize for character count and policy compliance, not brand differentiation or positioning strategy. If you accept AI rewrites blindly, you risk generic titles that read like commodity listings.

**How brand-registered sellers control AI rewrites:**

1. **Enable Review Listings Changes notifications.** Go to Seller Central > Settings > Notification Preferences > Listing Quality and Suppressed Listing Alerts. Check "Product detail page change."
2. **When you receive an AI recommendation, compare it against your manual rewrite.** Amazon's AI often strips brand voice and replaces differentiators with generic feature lists.
3. **Modify the AI recommendation before approving.** You can edit titles and Item Highlights in the Review Listings Changes dashboard.
4. **Approve or reject within 14 days.** After 14 days, the AI recommendation goes live automatically.

**What non-brand-registered sellers should do:**

You do not get AI recommendations or review windows. Amazon will rewrite your titles without notice after July 27. Your only option is to proactively rewrite all non-compliant titles before the enforcement date.

If you are running a private-label brand but have not yet completed Brand Registry, prioritize enrollment before July 27. The 14-day review window is the only way to control how Amazon AI rewrites your catalog.

**Common AI rewrite failures observed in early test batches:**

- Removing brand name to make room for features
- Replacing unique product names with generic descriptors (e.g., "ProGrip" becomes "Non-Slip")
- Dropping use-case keywords in favor of material keywords (e.g., "for Yoga" becomes "PVC")
- Reordering title structure in a way that breaks mobile readability

If your brand positioning depends on specific language (e.g., "clinical-grade," "barista-quality," "studio-tested"), manually rewrite titles to preserve that language before Amazon AI strips it.

## How This Fits Into Multi-Channel Listing Strategy (Amazon + Walmart + Shopify)

Amazon's 75-character title limit does not apply to Walmart or Shopify, but mobile-first title structure is becoming standard across all eCommerce platforms.

**Walmart Marketplace:** Walmart recommends 50–75 characters for product names, with a 200-character hard cap. You can reuse your Amazon 75-character titles on Walmart without modification. Walmart does not have an equivalent Item Highlights field, so move supporting details into the short description (up to 1,000 characters).

**Shopify / DTC:** No character limit, but Google Shopping and Facebook Catalog ads truncate titles at ~70 characters in ad preview. Use your Amazon title structure as the base, then extend it for SEO on your Shopify product page (e.g., add long-tail keywords in the full title that appear in on-page H1 tags but not in the meta title).

**Cross-channel workflow:**

1. Write the 75-character Amazon title first. This is your mobile-optimized core.
2. Reuse it on Walmart.
3. Extend it by 50–100 characters for Shopify on-page SEO, adding secondary keywords and use cases that do not fit in Amazon Item Highlights.
4. Use the same 75-character title in Google Shopping and Facebook Catalog feeds to ensure mobile ad preview consistency.

This approach reduces content creation overhead while maintaining keyword coverage and mobile readability across all channels. ## FAQ

**Does the 75-character limit apply to all Amazon categories?**

No. The 75-character limit applies to all categories **except media**: books, music, and video. Media titles can still exceed 75 characters. All other categories, including Toys, Home & Kitchen, Sports, Beauty, and Grocery, must comply by July 27, 2026.

**What happens if I don't rewrite my titles before July 27?**

Amazon's AI will gradually rewrite non-compliant titles after July 27. Brand-registered sellers receive AI-generated recommendations and have 14 days to review, modify, or reject them. Non-brand-registered sellers do not get advance notice, and titles are rewritten automatically without review. You can still edit your titles after Amazon rewrites them, but you risk temporary rank volatility and conversion drops during the AI rewrite period.

**Are Item Highlights indexed for search like titles and bullets?**

Yes. Amazon confirmed that Item Highlights are searchable and indexed. Keywords in Item Highlights contribute to organic ranking, similar to titles and bullet points. The 125-character Item Highlights field is not a substitute for bullets but an additional searchable content layer that appears on the detail page alongside the title.

**Can I use the same title structure on Walmart and Shopify?**

Yes. Walmart recommends 50–75 characters for product names, so Amazon's 75-character titles work natively on Walmart. Shopify has no character limit, but Google Shopping and Facebook Catalog ads truncate titles at ~70 characters in mobile previews, making the 75-character structure mobile-optimized across all platforms. You can extend Shopify titles for on-page SEO by adding secondary keywords after the core 75 characters.

**How do I prioritize which ASINs to rewrite first?**

Sort your catalog by last 30-day revenue and prioritize the top 20%. These are your highest-risk ASINs where AI rewrites could immediately impact conversion. For the top 20%, rewrite manually before July 27 and test on a small batch first. For the next 30%, use AI recommendations if brand-registered and review within 14 days. For the bottom 50%, accept AI rewrites or batch-edit using a template if you have capacity. Multiple agency operators recommend completing high-revenue SKU rewrites by July 20 to allow a stabilization window before enforcement begins.
