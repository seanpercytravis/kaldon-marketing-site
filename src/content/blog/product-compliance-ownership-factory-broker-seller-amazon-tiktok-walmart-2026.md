## The Compliance Ownership Problem in 2026

The seller or legal entity placing the product on the market owns compliance liability, not the factory or broker. Factories prepare test reports and certificates, freight forwarders handle customs documentation, customs brokers transmit data, and sellers maintain master records and sign declarations. A single CPSC lab report and Children's Product Certificate can be reused across Amazon and TikTok Shop when the same product and standards apply, but each platform requires specific formatting, importer details, and attribute matching. The hardest part is not obtaining one certificate—it is maintaining a dependable, SKU-level record for every product version across every marketplace.

Amazon now requires notified ASINs in categories such as dietary supplements, children's toys, and e-mobility products to be verified through Amazon-authorized testing, inspection, and certification providers. Sellers no longer submit documents directly to Amazon; the provider submits them. This materially shifts compliance ownership toward brands, manufacturers, and their designated testing partners. As of October 29, 2026, Amazon operates product verification at the child-ASIN level, may require samples or test-request forms, and can take about 30 days after a provider receives samples.

French authorities found 75% of more than 600 products sampled from seven foreign ecommerce platforms failed EU safety or compliance standards, with 46% both non-compliant and dangerous. The EU Product Liability Directive expands scrutiny across manufacturers, distributors, importers, retailers, and software providers, while marketplaces increasingly face obligations for unlawful or misleading products. This is not manufacturer-only ownership. This is distributed responsibility across every party that touches the product or its data.

## Who Prepares, Who Signs, Who Bears Liability: Decision Matrix

Compliance ownership is operational, not rhetorical. Every document has a preparer, a signer, and a liable party. The following matrix maps responsibility across Children's Product Certificates (CPC), test reports, certificates of conformity, declarations of conformity, and customs documents.

| Document | Who prepares | Who signs | Who is liable | Platform requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Children's Product Certificate (CPC)** | Factory or seller, based on lab test report | Seller or importer | Seller or importer | Amazon, TikTok Shop (children's products) |
| **Lab test report** | Accredited third-party lab (CPSC-accepted for U.S.) | Laboratory | Laboratory for test accuracy; seller for product compliance | Amazon, TikTok Shop, Walmart (category-dependent) |
| **Certificate of Conformity (EU)** | Factory or manufacturer | Manufacturer | Manufacturer, importer, or distributor (depending on jurisdiction) | Walmart International, eBay EU |
| **Declaration of Conformity (DoC)** | Factory or seller, based on internal assessment or third-party report | Seller or importer | Seller or importer | Amazon (certain categories), TikTok Shop (electronics, toys) |
| **Customs declaration (CBP 7501, CN22/CN23, etc.)** | Freight forwarder or seller | Importer of record | Importer of record | Required for all cross-border shipments |
| **ACE CPSC certificate transmission (U.S.)** | Customs broker or importer | Importer | Importer (even when broker transmits) | U.S. CPSC eFiling for regulated products |
| **Commercial invoice** | Seller or freight forwarder | Seller or exporter | Seller or exporter | All platforms, customs authorities |
| **Packing list** | Factory or freight forwarder | Factory or freight forwarder | No legal liability; operational accuracy only | Customs, freight forwarders |

The U.S. CPSC eFiling expansion to international-mail shipments, effective October 22, 2026, makes compliance ownership operationally explicit: importers remain responsible for required certificate information, even when brokers, carriers, logistics providers, or overseas sellers transmit the data. The implementation guidance highlights accurate trade-party records, CPSC laboratory identifiers, certificate-update logic, API/CSV validation, and a 5,000-character certificate limit.

**Key takeaway:** The importer of record (usually the seller) is the liable party for nearly every compliance document except factory-prepared lab reports and packing lists. Factories prepare and sign lab test reports. Sellers or importers sign CPCs, DoCs, and customs declarations. Customs brokers transmit data on behalf of the importer but do not assume liability.

## Which Documents Can Be Reused Across Amazon, TikTok Shop, and Walmart

A compliance package prepared for one marketplace can often support another when the same product, standards, and importer details apply. The following table maps reusability.

| Document | Amazon | TikTok Shop | Walmart | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CPSC-accepted lab test report** | Yes | Yes | Yes (category-dependent) | Same product, same lab, same standard. Report must include product identifier, test standard, lab accreditation, and results. |
| **Children's Product Certificate (CPC)** | Yes | Yes | Yes (category-dependent) | Must list the correct importer name, address, and contact. Platform-specific formatting not required, but importer details must match platform account. |
| **Certificate of Conformity (EU)** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Reusable across platforms when the same manufacturer and product apply. |
| **Declaration of Conformity (DoC)** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Signer must be the legal entity responsible for the listing on each platform. If different entities list the product on different platforms, each entity signs its own DoC. |
| **Safety Data Sheet (SDS)** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Same chemical composition, same SDS. Platform-specific versions not required unless jurisdiction changes (e.g., U.S. OSHA SDS vs. EU CLP SDS). |
| **Commercial invoice** | No | No | No | Platform-irrelevant; prepared per shipment for customs. |
| **Product images and descriptions** | No | No | No | Each platform has different attribute requirements, image formats, and listing structures. Content cannot be copy-pasted. |

**Reusability rule:** Lab reports, certificates, and declarations can be reused when the product, standard, and signer are the same. Listing content, invoices, and platform-specific data cannot.

Beginning November 1, 2026, every low-value EU ecommerce parcel requires a product identifier on its customs declaration, and the carrier depends on the brand's catalog for a correct identifier. This means product data and compliance data are now operationally connected. A missing or incorrect GTIN, EAN, or ASIN can delay customs clearance even when the product itself is compliant.

## Platform-Specific Compliance Formatting and Attribute Requirements

While the underlying compliance evidence can be reused, each platform has different formatting, attribute, and submission requirements.

### Amazon

- **Submission method:** Direct upload to Seller Central for most categories. For notified ASINs (as of October 29, 2026), Amazon-authorized testing providers submit documents directly to Amazon.
- **Required attributes:** Product identifier (ASIN, UPC, EAN), manufacturer name and address, importer name and address, product description, test standard, lab name and accreditation, test date, certificate issue date.
- **Dangerous goods:** Beginning October 26, 2026, Amazon requires technical compliance answers when sellers create or edit listings for refrigerators/air conditioners/freezers, shock absorbers and suspension products, markers and writing instruments, and pool/spa chemicals. Required information can include refrigerant type and mass, ink properties, chemical composition, and packaging details.
- **Insurance:** Amazon is removing the prior sales-volume floor for sellers in eleven high-risk categories, including children's products, supplements, cosmetics, food and beverages, pet consumables, lithium-battery devices, medical devices, OTC health products, automotive parts, safety equipment, and household chemicals. The reported requirement is at least $1 million per occurrence, with product-liability and completed-operations coverage; noncompliant listings may be suppressed beginning November 2, 2026.

### TikTok Shop

- **Submission method:** Upload to Seller Center during listing creation or category qualification. Some restricted categories require approval before listing.
- **Required attributes:** Product name, brand, manufacturer, importer (if applicable), country of origin, HS code, applicable certifications. Documentation must match the exact listing, including product identity, images, descriptions, and country-of-origin information.
- **Restricted categories:** Dietary supplements, children's products, electronics, cosmetics, and medical devices require category- or product-level qualification before listing. A product cannot simply be listed first and documented later; approval may be required before sale.
- **Prop 65:** Missing Prop 65 evidence or warnings can lead to listing deactivation, account penalties, refunds, or loss of selling access. When TikTok Shop asks for chemical evidence, sellers must either provide testing or update the listing and packaging with the required warning.
- **EPR:** Compliance ownership now extends beyond safety certificates into recycling registrations and market-by-market reporting. EPR obligations shift responsibility toward the seller or entity first placing products on a market, including registration, reporting, and fees.

For a detailed breakdown of TikTok Shop category qualification, see [TikTok Shop Category Qualification & Compliance Checklist 2026](/blog/tiktok-shop-category-qualification-compliance-checklist-2026).

### Walmart

- **Submission method:** Upload to Walmart Seller Center. For high-risk categories, Walmart may request certificates during onboarding or listing review.
- **Required attributes:** Product identifier (GTIN, UPC), manufacturer, importer, country of origin, applicable certifications. Walmart's listing structure requires more granular attribute mapping than Amazon or TikTok Shop.
- **Enforcement:** FDA warned Walmart over unapproved drugs sold through Walmart.com and Walmart Fulfillment Services on October 1, 2026. The products were purchased on Walmart.com and introduced into interstate commerce through Walmart Fulfillment Services, highlighting potential platform and fulfillment liability for marketplace operators and the need for stronger seller-product verification.

**Platform-specific formatting rule:** The same lab report can support all three platforms, but the listing must include platform-required attributes (ASIN for Amazon, GTIN for Walmart, exact product name for TikTok Shop) and the importer name must match the account name on each platform.

## How to Build a SKU-Level Compliance Record That Survives Audits and Platform Changes

Maintaining accurate, current, SKU-level documentation across marketplaces and jurisdictions is the most visible operational pain. The hard part is not obtaining one certificate—it is keeping a dependable record for every SKU and version.

A dependable compliance record includes:

1. **Lab test report** (PDF, stored in a version-controlled folder or compliance platform)
2. **Certificate** (CPC, CoC, or DoC, signed by the importer or manufacturer)
3. **Importer details** (legal name, address, phone, email)
4. **Product attributes** (GTIN, UPC, EAN, ASIN, HS code, product name, brand, manufacturer)
5. **Platform-specific versions** (Amazon ASIN, TikTok Shop product ID, Walmart item number)
6. **Effective dates** (certificate issue date, test date, expiration date if applicable)
7. **Regulatory references** (16 CFR 1201 for safety glazing, ASTM F963 for toys, 21 CFR 175.300 for food-contact materials, etc.)
8. **Supplier contact** (factory name, address, contact person, date of last verification)

For suppliers claiming FSC, food-contact material, or other certifications, see [Verify Supplier Compliance Certificates: FSC & Food-Contact Material Validation Workflow 2026](/blog/verify-supplier-compliance-certificates-fsc-food-contact-material-validation-workflow-2026).

**Storage options:**

- **Spreadsheet** (Google Sheets or Excel): Works for catalogs under 50 SKUs. Requires manual version control and file-naming discipline.
- **Cloud folder** (Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive): Works for up to 200 SKUs if folder structure is clear (e.g., `/compliance/[SKU]/[document-type]/[version].pdf`).
- **Compliance platform** (dedicated SaaS): Required for catalogs over 200 SKUs, multi-marketplace sellers, or agencies managing client launches. Features include certificate expiration alerts, platform-specific export, supplier tracking, and API integrations.
- **PIM with compliance module** (Akeneo, Salsify, etc.): Enterprise solution. Combines product data, compliance records, and channel syndication. Akeneo's October 28, 2026 Autumn Release positions agent permissions, traceable changes, approval gates, readiness policies, and role-based access as essential when AI touches "pricing, compliance and brand" data.

**Update trigger events:**

- Product formula or material change
- Supplier change
- Regulatory standard update (e.g., ASTM F963-23 replaces ASTM F963-17)
- Marketplace policy change (e.g., Amazon adds dangerous-goods questions)
- Platform account change (different legal entity, different importer address)
- Recall or safety alert

For recalled or safety-alerted ASINs, Amazon may suppress listings, make inventory unsellable, cancel orders, notify customers, and require removal or destruction of inventory within 30 days. Reinstatement may require a manufacturer's Letter of Compliance and evidence that regulatory issues have been resolved. This makes recall ownership a cross-functional responsibility spanning brand, manufacturer, importer, regulatory, and marketplace teams.

## Factory vs. Broker vs. Seller: Who Does What in a Real Launch Workflow

The following is a step-by-step workflow for a U.S. seller launching a silicone baby spoon (children's product, food-contact material) sourced from a Guangdong factory and selling on Amazon, TikTok Shop, and Walmart.

### Step 1: Factory prepares samples and arranges lab testing

- **Who:** Factory
- **Deliverable:** Product samples shipped to CPSC-accepted lab
- **Timeline:** 3–5 days for sampling, 7–14 days for lab testing
- **Cost:** $300–$800 per test (ASTM F963 for mechanical/physical, 16 CFR 1501 for small parts, 21 CFR 175.300 for food-contact materials)

### Step 2: Lab issues test report

- **Who:** CPSC-accepted third-party lab
- **Deliverable:** Test report (PDF) listing product description, test standard, test results, lab accreditation, and test date
- **Timeline:** 7–14 days from sample receipt
- **Cost:** Included in step 1

### Step 3: Seller prepares and signs Children's Product Certificate (CPC)

- **Who:** Seller (importer of record)
- **Deliverable:** CPC (PDF or digital record) listing product identifier, applicable regulations (CPSIA, ASTM F963, 16 CFR 1501, 21 CFR 175.300), manufacturer name and address, importer name and address, lab name and accreditation, test date, and certificate issue date
- **Timeline:** 1 day
- **Cost:** $0 (self-prepared) or $50–$150 (compliance consultant)

For multi-platform supplier verification, see [Supplier Verification for Multi-Platform Compliance: Amazon, Walmart & TikTok Shop 2026](/blog/supplier-verification-multi-platform-compliance-amazon-walmart-tiktok-2026).

### Step 4: Seller creates listings on Amazon, TikTok Shop, and Walmart

- **Who:** Seller or agency
- **Deliverable:** Three platform listings with product attributes, images, descriptions, and compliance fields populated
- **Timeline:** 1–3 days per platform (if no qualification delays)
- **Cost:** $0 (self-service) or $200–$500 per platform (agency)

**Platform-specific notes:**

- **Amazon:** Upload CPC and lab report to Seller Central. For notified ASINs, work with Amazon-authorized testing provider.
- **TikTok Shop:** Upload CPC and lab report during category qualification. Ensure product name, brand, manufacturer, and importer match the CPC exactly.
- **Walmart:** Upload CPC and lab report to Seller Center. Ensure GTIN matches the product packaging.

### Step 5: Freight forwarder arranges shipment and prepares customs documentation

- **Who:** Freight forwarder
- **Deliverable:** Commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, customs declaration (CBP 7501 for U.S.)
- **Timeline:** 1–2 days
- **Cost:** $150–$300 (included in freight quote)

### Step 6: Customs broker transmits CPSC certificate data via ACE eFiling

- **Who:** Customs broker (acting on behalf of importer)
- **Deliverable:** CPSC certificate data transmitted to U.S. Customs and Border Protection via ACE
- **Timeline:** Same day as customs clearance
- **Cost:** $50–$150 per shipment
- **Liability:** Importer (even though broker transmits)

The U.S. CPSC eFiling expansion to international-mail shipments, effective October 22, 2026, applies to regulated products entering via international mail. Importers remain responsible for required certificate information, even when brokers, carriers, logistics providers, or overseas sellers transmit the data.

### Step 7: Seller maintains master compliance record

- **Who:** Seller
- **Deliverable:** Folder or platform entry containing lab report, CPC, product attributes, platform-specific versions, effective dates, and supplier contact
- **Timeline:** Ongoing (updated whenever product, supplier, or regulation changes)
- **Cost:** $0 (manual spreadsheet) or $50–$200/month (compliance platform)

**Total cost for one SKU across three platforms:** $650–$2,050 (one-time) + $50–$200/month (ongoing record maintenance).

## Common Mistakes That Create Liability Gaps

1. **Factory signs the CPC instead of the importer.** The importer of record must sign the CPC. A factory-signed CPC is not valid for U.S. import or Amazon/TikTok Shop/Walmart compliance.
2. **Broker listed as importer on the CPC.** The customs broker transmits data on behalf of the importer but is not the importer of record. The seller or brand entity is the importer.
3. **Lab report lists a different product than the one being sold.** Product description, color, material, and dimensions must match the listing. A silicone spoon tested in pink cannot be sold in blue without a separate test.
4. **CPC lists the wrong platform entity.** If Seller LLC lists products on Amazon and TikTok Shop but Seller Inc. lists on Walmart, each entity must issue its own CPC with its own name and address.
5. **Certificate expires or standard is updated.** ASTM F963-17 was replaced by ASTM F963-23. A CPC listing the old standard is no longer valid.
6. **Missing platform-specific attributes.** A valid CPC and lab report do not guarantee listing approval if the listing is missing required attributes (refrigerant type, ink properties, chemical composition, HS code, etc.).
7. **No record of who prepared, signed, or updated the document.** When a platform or regulator asks "who is responsible for this certificate," the answer must be traceable.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is legally responsible for a Children's Product Certificate: the factory or the seller?

The seller or importer of record is legally responsible and must sign the CPC. The factory prepares the lab test report, but the importer signs the certificate, maintains the record, and bears liability.

### Can I use the same lab test report for Amazon, TikTok Shop, and Walmart?

Yes, when the same product, lab, and standard apply. The lab report must include product identifier, test standard, lab accreditation, and results. Platform-specific formatting is not required, but listing attributes must match the report.

### What happens if my customs broker is listed as the importer on my compliance documents?

The CPC is invalid. The customs broker transmits data on behalf of the importer but is not the importer of record. The seller or brand entity must be listed as the importer on all compliance certificates.

### Do I need separate compliance packages for each marketplace?

No, the underlying lab reports and certificates can be reused when the product, standard, and signer are the same. However, each platform requires specific listing attributes, and the importer name must match the account entity on each platform.
