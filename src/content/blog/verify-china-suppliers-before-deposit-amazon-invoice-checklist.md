## Why Supplier Verification Now Means Two Separate Checklists

Verification means two things: prepayment fraud protection (GSXT business license cross-check, live factory proof, bank validation) and invoice survivability for Amazon compliance and long-term scaling. The problem is that most verification guides focus only on existence checks: does the factory have a business license? Can they show a warehouse video? That stops deposit fraud. ## Prepayment Fraud Protection: Five Checks Before the Wire

Before you send a deposit, verify five data points. This is not about trust. It is about cross-checking claims against public records and behavior signals that scammers cannot fake at scale.

### 1. GSXT Business License Cross-Check

Every legitimate Chinese company has a business license registered with the State Administration for Market Regulation. The public database is GSXT (gsxt.gov.cn). You can search by company name or Unified Social Credit Code.

What to verify: registered business name matches the entity on your proforma invoice, registration date is not within the last 90 days (new shells are a fraud flag), registered capital is proportional to the order size you are discussing, business scope includes your product category, and the legal representative name appears consistently across communications.

If the supplier gives you a business license PDF, cross-check it on GSXT. If they refuse to provide a Unified Social Credit Code, stop. A June 2026 r/Alibaba post laying out first-time sourcing measures emphasizes GSXT checks as the first mandatory step, with commenters noting that fake PDFs are trivial to produce but GSXT records are not.

### 2. Live Factory Proof (Video Walkthrough, Not Stock Photos)

Request a live video walkthrough of the production floor and warehouse, showing your specific product or similar SKUs in process. Stock photos and generic factory tours are easily borrowed. A live video call with a supervisor who can answer technical questions about your product is harder to fake.

What to verify: machinery matches the manufacturing process for your product, the factory has raw materials and semi-finished goods on hand (not an empty shell), staff are present and working (not a fake backdrop), the factory can show quality control stations and testing equipment, and the supervisor can answer specific questions about lead time, MOQ, and customization without hesitation.

A June 2026 guide for AI-found suppliers explicitly warns that factory proof videos do not replace formal due diligence, but they do screen out trading companies renting office space and posing as manufacturers.

### 3. Bank Account Validation (Corporate Account, Not Personal)

Wire to a corporate bank account that matches the business license. Never wire to a personal account, Western Union, or payment apps. Scammers prefer untraceable transfers.

What to verify: the account holder name matches the registered business name on GSXT, the bank is a major Chinese commercial bank (ICBC, Bank of China, China Construction Bank, Agricultural Bank of China), and the supplier provides a formal contract or proforma invoice on company letterhead referencing the bank account.

If the supplier insists on Western Union or a personal account for a deposit, stop. A current r/Alibaba checklist explicitly flags personal accounts as a deal-breaker.

### 4. Third-Party Trade Data Check

Use a trade intelligence tool (Panjiva, ImportGenius, Verisupplier) to confirm the supplier has an export history. Scammers and inexperienced trading companies will have no customs records or only recent, small shipments.

What to verify: the supplier has shipped your product category before, they have multiple export destinations (not just one buyer, which could be internal shell activity), shipment volume is consistent with the factory size they claim, and recent shipments (last 12 months) exist.

A June 2026 trend signal from Made-in-China.com's new SourcingAI platform shows that access to verified supplier networks and exclusive China trade data is now a bundled value proposition for AI sourcing tools, reflecting rising demand for export-history verification.

### 5. Reference Check (Existing Customer, Verifiable Contact)

Ask for two references from customers in your region (US, EU, AU). Contact them. A fake reference will give you a phone number that goes to the supplier's team or a generic email.

What to verify: the reference customer is a real business with a web presence, they placed multiple orders (not just one sample), they can describe product quality, lead time accuracy, and communication responsiveness, and they are willing to share photos of received goods or invoices.

If the supplier cannot provide verifiable references or the references feel scripted, that is a yellow flag. Not every new factory will have US customers, but established exporters will.

## Invoice Survivability: Four Documentation Standards for Scaling

Once you confirm the supplier is real, the second verification layer is invoice and documentation quality. This determines whether your product clears customs smoothly, survives Amazon compliance reviews, and allows clean expansion to Walmart, Shopify, or international markets.

### 1. Commercial Invoice Must Match Landed Cost and Classification

Your commercial invoice (the document used for customs clearance) must reflect the actual landed cost of goods. Undervaluing goods to reduce import duties is customs fraud. It also creates a mismatch between your invoices, your freight forwarder documents, and your bookkeeping, which surfaces during audits, tax filings, or financing applications.

What to verify: the per-unit price on the commercial invoice matches the price on your proforma invoice and purchase order, freight and insurance are either included in the unit price or broken out separately (depending on your Incoterm), the HS code (Harmonized System classification) is correct for your product, and the supplier can provide a detailed packing list that matches the invoice.

Amazon compliance teams sometimes request commercial invoices during brand registry reviews or when investigating counterfeit claims. If your invoice shows $2 per unit but your retail price is $40, you may be flagged for reselling counterfeits or low-quality goods.

### 2. Product Certificates Must Be Model-Specific and Market-Specific

Generic certificates are worthless. You need product testing and certification documents that reference your exact model, your destination market, and the applicable standards (UL, FCC, CE, FDA, CPSIA, Prop 65).

What to verify: the certificate lists your product model number or a model the supplier can demonstrate is identical to yours, the testing lab is accredited (you can verify lab accreditation on ILAC or the FCC database for electronics), the certificate covers the destination market you are selling in (a CE certificate is not valid for US sales), and the issue date is recent (certifications expire, especially for electronics and children's products).

A 2026 compliance guide for pet product importers highlights that FDA has issued Import Alerts affecting over 340 product lines from Chinese manufacturers for food contact violations, and that importers must verify test report numbers directly with the issuing laboratory because document fraud is common.

If you are selling on Amazon in categories like electronics, kids products, or consumables, you may be required to upload certificates to Seller Central. If the certificates are fake or generic, your listing will be suppressed and your account flagged.

### 3. Consistent Entity Name Across All Documents

Your supplier's business name must match across the business license, commercial invoice, proforma invoice, freight documents, and bank account. If the proforma invoice says "Shenzhen ABC Manufacturing Co., Ltd." but the commercial invoice says "Guangzhou ABC Trading," you have a trading company intermediary or a documentation mismatch that will cause customs delays and compliance issues.

What to verify: the business name on every document matches the GSXT registration, the legal representative name is consistent, and the supplier can explain any name variation (for example, a parent company vs subsidiary) with supporting corporate structure documents.

Inconsistent entity names are a red flag for Amazon Brand Registry, customs, and freight forwarders. They also make it harder to enforce contracts or file claims if product quality fails.

### 4. Payment Terms and Milestones Are Written Into the Purchase Order

Your purchase order (PO) should specify payment terms (30/60/10 is common: 30% deposit, 60% before shipment, 10% after inspection), inspection rights (you or a third party can inspect goods before final payment), penalties for late delivery or quality defects, and product specifications (materials, dimensions, certifications, packaging).

What to verify: the PO is signed and stamped by both parties, the supplier references the PO number on invoices and shipping documents, and the payment milestones align with your cash flow and risk tolerance.

A June 2026 r/Alibaba post recommends keeping a quality holdback (not paying 100% until after inspection) as a critical risk-reduction move. This also gives you leverage if the supplier tries to substitute materials or rush production.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the GSXT database and how do I use it to verify a China supplier?

GSXT (gsxt.gov.cn) is the public business registration database for Chinese companies. Search by company name or Unified Social Credit Code to confirm the supplier's registered business name, registration date, registered capital, business scope, and legal representative match what they told you. If the supplier refuses to provide a code or the details do not match, stop.

### Should I pay a deposit to a supplier who only accepts Western Union or a personal bank account?

No. Always wire to a corporate bank account that matches the business license. Personal accounts and Western Union are untraceable and preferred by scammers. A legitimate supplier will have a corporate account at a major Chinese bank (ICBC, Bank of China, China Construction Bank, Agricultural Bank of China).

### What documents do I need from a China supplier to pass Amazon compliance reviews?

You need a commercial invoice that matches your landed cost and HS code, model-specific product certificates (UL, FCC, CE, CPSIA, Prop 65) for your destination market from accredited labs, and consistent entity names across all documents (invoice, freight docs, bank account). Generic certificates or inconsistent names will trigger compliance flags.

### How do I verify a supplier has real export experience before I place an order?

Use a trade intelligence tool (Panjiva, ImportGenius, Verisupplier) to check customs records. Confirm the supplier has shipped your product category before, has multiple export destinations, and has recent shipments in the last 12 months. No export history or only recent small shipments is a red flag.
