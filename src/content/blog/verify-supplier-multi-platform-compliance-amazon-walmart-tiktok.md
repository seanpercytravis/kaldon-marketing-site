## Why Platform-Specific Compliance Now Blocks Multi-Channel Expansion

Most supplier verification guides are channel-agnostic, but Amazon, Walmart, and TikTok Shop now enforce platform-specific compliance requirements (invoices, authorization letters, brand registry, counterfeit programs) that act as gatekeepers. This article provides a pre-expansion checklist to validate suppliers can meet these requirements before you commit inventory or switch channels, especially critical now that TikTok Shop anti-counterfeit enforcement and Amazon's March 2026 end to commingled inventory have raised traceability stakes.

Amazon ended commingled inventory in all U.S. fulfillment centers effective March 31, 2026. Every unit now requires individual FNSKU tracking. Walmart Fulfillment Services introduced a long-term storage fee (450+ days) on June 30, 2026. TikTok Shop implemented new guidelines prohibiting AI-generated voices and static-image livestreams in June 2026, signaling a broader trust and compliance shift. The FDA issued a series of Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP) warning letters in recent months, and Amazon expanded cGMP compliance requirements to all dietary supplements. These are not one-off events. They are part of a compliance ratchet that punishes sellers who expand without validating supplier readiness.

The dominant pattern among serious sellers in 2026 is moving from single-platform checks (Alibaba profile looks fine) to **multi-platform and multi-signal verification**: curated directories, corporate registry lookups, physical address validation, trade show exhibitor lists, references, and third-party quality inspections. Alibaba's 1688.com AI supplier-verification engine audited 2 million supplier profiles and cut due-diligence time by 40 percent, framed as "end-to-end, automated supplier checks across all marketplace listings." Procurement teams now ask for "multi-market supplier verification" that reconciles risk signals across Amazon Business, Alibaba, regional marketplaces, and direct vendor portals, not just one platform.

If your supplier cannot produce the documents, partner with the labs, or navigate the platform policies outlined below, you will hit compliance walls after you have already moved inventory, signed contracts, or launched listings. This checklist prevents that.

## Pre-Expansion Compliance Checklist by Platform

### Amazon

**What Amazon requires before and during listing:**

1. **Invoices with matching supplier details.** Amazon can request supply-chain verification on tight timelines. Invoices must show the supplier's legal business name, address, contact information, itemized product descriptions, quantities, and dates. The supplier name on the invoice must match corporate registry records and any authorization letters. If your supplier is a trading company, Amazon may ask for manufacturer invoices upstream.
2. **Authorization letters for branded products.** If you sell products under a registered brand (yours or a licensed brand), Amazon requires a letter from the brand owner or manufacturer authorizing you to sell. The letter must be on company letterhead, signed, dated, and include the brand name, ASIN or product description, your seller name, and contact details for verification.
3. **Brand Registry enrollment (if you own the brand).** Amazon Brand Registry requires an active registered trademark in the country where you sell. Your supplier must be able to produce packaging, labels, and product documentation that match your trademark exactly. Inconsistent branding or generic labels can trigger authenticity complaints.
4. **cGMP compliance for supplements.** Amazon now requires all dietary supplements to show cGMP compliance verified by an accredited third-party TIC organization. Your supplier must maintain certifications and lab test reports you can submit on demand.
5. **FNSKU labeling capability.** Commingling ended March 31, 2026. Every unit shipped to FBA must carry a unique FNSKU label. Your supplier must apply labels correctly or ship to a prep center that can. Mislabeling or missing labels delay inventory receipt and hurt your IPI score.
6. **Category-specific compliance.** Topicals, hazmat, FDA-regulated products, and restricted categories require additional documentation (Safety Data Sheets, FDA registration, test reports). Confirm your supplier has worked with these requirements before and can produce documents in English.

**How to verify:**

Request sample invoices and authorization letters before placing your first order. Check that the supplier's legal name on the invoice matches their business registration (use local corporate registries or services like Dun & Bradstreet). If the supplier has never shipped to Amazon FBA, ask for references from other Amazon sellers in your category. Run a test shipment with FNSKU labels applied and confirm receipt in Seller Central before committing to bulk orders.

For regulated products, request copies of certifications and lab reports. Cross-check lab accreditation with ISO 17025 databases or the ANSI National Accreditation Board. If the supplier cannot produce these in 48 hours, they are not ready for Amazon.

### Walmart

**What Walmart requires before and during listing:**

1. **Invoices with item-level detail.** Walmart Marketplace enforces invoice verification for brand-gated and high-risk categories. Invoices must show the supplier's legal business name, address, itemized product descriptions, quantities, unit prices, and dates. Walmart cross-checks supplier information during application review and post-launch audits.
2. **Authorization letters for gated brands.** Walmart maintains a brand gate for thousands of national brands. If you apply to sell a gated brand, you must submit an authorization letter from the brand owner or an invoice from an authorized distributor. The letter must be signed, dated, and on company letterhead.
3. **Supplier W-9 or W-8 (for U.S. and international suppliers).** Walmart requires tax documentation for payment and compliance. Your supplier must provide a completed W-9 (U.S. entities) or W-8 (non-U.S. entities) that matches their legal business name and EIN or tax ID. Mismatches trigger payment holds.
4. **UPC and GTIN compliance.** Walmart requires valid GTINs for most categories. Your supplier must source products with legitimate UPCs from GS1 or an authorized reseller. Recycled, reused, or fake UPCs result in listing suppression.
5. **Walmart Fulfillment Services (WFS) prep requirements.** If you use WFS, your supplier must meet Walmart's packaging, labeling, and case-pack standards. Products must arrive in scannable condition with correct SKU labels. WFS introduced a long-term storage fee for inventory held longer than 450 days effective June 30, 2026, so your supplier's lead times and MOQs must align with turnover expectations.
6. **Supplier diversity and sustainability documentation (optional but preferred).** Walmart prioritizes suppliers with diversity certifications (WBENC, NMSDC) and sustainability commitments. While not mandatory for all sellers, having a supplier who can produce these documents improves application approval and future category access.

**How to verify:**

Request a sample invoice and W-9 or W-8 before onboarding. Confirm the supplier's legal name and tax ID match their corporate registration. If the supplier is a distributor, ask for proof of authorized distribution (letter from the manufacturer or invoice from the brand owner). For WFS, request packaging specifications and confirm the supplier has experience shipping to U.S. fulfillment centers with Walmart or Amazon. Run a test order to validate prep quality before committing to WFS inventory.

For gated brands, confirm your supplier is an authorized distributor by contacting the brand directly. Walmart enforces brand gates aggressively and suspends sellers who submit fraudulent authorization letters.

### TikTok Shop

**What TikTok Shop requires before and during listing:**

1. **Invoices with supplier and product traceability.** TikTok Shop enforces anti-counterfeit policies through invoice verification. Invoices must show the supplier's legal business name, address, itemized product descriptions, quantities, and dates. TikTok cross-checks supplier information during seller onboarding and can request invoices post-launch.
2. **Authorization letters for branded products.** TikTok Shop requires authorization letters for products sold under national or registered brands. The letter must be signed, dated, on company letterhead, and include the brand name, product description, your seller name, and contact details for verification.
3. **Product authenticity guarantees.** TikTok Shop enforces a zero-tolerance counterfeit policy. Your supplier must provide documentation proving product authenticity (invoices from the brand owner, manufacturer certificates, or authorized distributor agreements). TikTok may suspend your shop and withhold funds if counterfeit products are reported.
4. **Real-time livestream compliance.** TikTok Shop implemented new guidelines in June 2026 prohibiting AI-generated voices and static-image livestreams. Your supplier's product presentation, packaging, and labeling must support real-time, authentic creator content. If your supplier ships generic or mislabeled products, you cannot run compliant live selling.
5. **Supplier contact information for verification.** TikTok Shop may contact your supplier directly to verify invoices and authorization letters. Your supplier must respond within 48 hours or your shop may be suspended. Confirm your supplier has English-speaking contacts and is prepared for platform outreach.
6. **Cross-border logistics and DDP shipping (if applicable).** TikTok Shop increasingly requires Delivered Duty Paid (DDP) shipping for cross-border sellers. Your supplier must calculate landed costs accurately and ship with tariffs prepaid. Etsy implemented similar DDP requirements for non-U.S. sellers effective July 9, 2026, signaling a broader trend.

**How to verify:**

Request sample invoices and authorization letters before listing on TikTok Shop. Confirm the supplier's legal name matches their business registration. If the supplier is a trading company, ask for manufacturer invoices upstream. Test the supplier's responsiveness by sending a mock verification request. If they cannot respond in English within 48 hours, they are not ready for TikTok Shop.

For branded products, contact the brand owner to confirm your supplier is authorized. TikTok Shop suspends sellers who submit fraudulent authorization letters and enforces bans across all accounts tied to the same entity.

For livestream compliance, order sample products and evaluate packaging, labeling, and presentation quality. If the product arrives with generic labels, misspellings, or no branding, you cannot run compliant creator content.

## Common Compliance Gaps That Block Multi-Channel Expansion

**1. Supplier legal name does not match invoices or authorization letters.**

This is the most common compliance failure. Your supplier's Alibaba profile says "Shenzhen ABC Trading Co., Ltd." but the invoice shows "Guangzhou XYZ Manufacturing Co., Ltd." Amazon, Walmart, and TikTok Shop cross-check supplier names against corporate registries and authorization letters. Mismatches trigger suspensions.

**How to fix:** Request a business license or corporate registration certificate before placing your first order. Confirm the legal business name and match it to all invoices and authorization letters. If the supplier is a trading company, ask for the manufacturer's legal name and invoice upstream.

**2. Supplier cannot produce authorization letters for branded products.**

If you source branded products (licensed or OEM), platforms require authorization letters from the brand owner. Many suppliers claim they can provide these letters but fail to deliver when asked. Fake or forged letters result in permanent suspensions.

**How to fix:** Request a sample authorization letter before placing your first order. Verify the letter with the brand owner directly (call the brand's customer service or email their wholesale department). Do not accept letters without signatures, dates, or company letterheads.

**3. Supplier has no experience with platform-specific labeling or packaging.**

Amazon FNSKU labels, Walmart case-pack standards, and TikTok Shop creator-friendly packaging require supplier coordination. Suppliers who have never shipped to these platforms make labeling errors that delay inventory receipt, hurt performance metrics, and block sales.

**How to fix:** Ask for references from other sellers who have used the supplier for Amazon FBA, Walmart WFS, or TikTok Shop. Request photos of labeled products before shipment. Run a test order to validate labeling accuracy before committing to bulk orders. If the supplier cannot apply labels correctly, budget for a U.S.-based prep center.

**4. Supplier cannot produce compliance documentation for regulated products.**

Supplements, topicals, FDA-regulated products, and hazmat require certifications, lab reports, Safety Data Sheets, and registration numbers. Suppliers who claim compliance but cannot produce documents within 48 hours are not ready for regulated categories.

**How to fix:** Request copies of certifications and lab reports before placing your first order. Cross-check lab accreditation with ISO 17025 databases or the ANSI National Accreditation Board. If the supplier hesitates or produces generic certificates, escalate due diligence or find a different supplier.

**5. Supplier's lead times and MOQs do not align with platform storage fees.**

Walmart introduced a long-term storage fee for inventory held longer than 450 days effective June 30, 2026. Amazon enforces IPI penalties for slow-moving inventory. If your supplier's MOQ is 5,000 units and your monthly sales velocity is 200 units, you will pay storage fees that erase your margin.

**How to fix:** Calculate your monthly sales velocity using historical data or demand forecasts from [Kaldon's Discover phase](https://kaldon.io/features). Match your supplier's MOQ to 90-120 days of inventory. Negotiate smaller initial orders or flexible reorder terms. If the supplier cannot accommodate lower MOQs, source from a different supplier or use a freight forwarder who can split shipments.

## When to Walk Away from a Supplier

**Red flags that indicate a supplier cannot handle multi-platform compliance:**

1. **Refuses to provide a business license or corporate registration certificate.** Legitimate suppliers provide these documents within 24-48 hours. Refusal or delay indicates the supplier is a middleman, unlicensed, or operating under a different legal entity.
2. **Legal name on invoices does not match Alibaba or trade show profiles.** This is a hard stop. Platforms cross-check supplier names against invoices and authorization letters. Mismatches trigger suspensions.
3. **Cannot produce authorization letters or offers to forge them.** Suppliers who offer to "create" authorization letters without brand owner involvement are frauds. Platforms verify authorization letters with brand owners. Fake letters result in permanent bans.
4. **No references from other Amazon, Walmart, or TikTok Shop sellers.** Suppliers who have never shipped to these platforms do not understand labeling, packaging, or compliance requirements. They will make errors that cost you time and money.
5. **Slow or evasive communication during sample orders.** If the supplier takes 5+ days to respond to compliance questions during a sample order, they will not respond faster during bulk orders or platform audits. Responsiveness during diligence predicts responsiveness under pressure.
6. **Pricing is 30-50 percent below market without explanation.** If the supplier's price is half the market rate and they cannot explain why (owned factory, vertical integration, exclusive material source), the product is likely counterfeit, B-grade, or misrepresented.

**When to escalate due diligence instead of walking away:**

If the supplier has strong references, trade show history, and compliance documentation but raises one or two yellow flags (slow initial communication, minor invoice formatting errors), escalate due diligence instead of walking away. Request a third-party factory inspection, video call with the factory manager, or in-person visit if you are in the region. Many legitimate suppliers have poor English communication or unfamiliarity with U.S. platform requirements but can deliver once requirements are clarified.

## Frequently asked questions

### What documents do I need from my supplier before listing on Amazon, Walmart, and TikTok Shop?

You need invoices with matching supplier legal names, authorization letters for branded products, business licenses or corporate registration certificates, certifications for regulated products (cGMP for supplements, FDA registration for topicals), and W-9 or W-8 tax forms for Walmart. Platforms cross-check these documents during onboarding and post-launch audits.

### How do I verify my supplier is an authorized distributor for a branded product?

Request an authorization letter from your supplier, then contact the brand owner directly (call customer service or email the wholesale department). Provide the supplier's legal business name and ask if they are an authorized distributor. If the brand owner does not recognize the supplier, you are sourcing from an unauthorized middleman.

### Can I use the same supplier for Amazon FBA, Walmart WFS, and TikTok Shop?

Yes, if the supplier can meet platform-specific labeling, packaging, and compliance requirements. Amazon requires FNSKU labels (commingling ended March 31, 2026), Walmart requires case-pack standards and GTINs, and TikTok Shop requires creator-friendly packaging and real-time livestream compliance. Run test shipments to each platform before committing to bulk orders.

### What are the biggest compliance mistakes sellers make when expanding to multiple platforms?

The most common mistakes are supplier legal names that do not match invoices or authorization letters, fake or forged authorization letters, suppliers with no experience applying platform-specific labels, missing compliance documentation for regulated products, and MOQs that do not align with platform storage fees. These errors trigger suspensions and inventory holds.
